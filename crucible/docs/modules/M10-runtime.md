- **Lab audit R-3 (2026-10-17):** a failed grade is graded again on the same work; the policy is never sampled again because a grade failed. Before R-3 the Runner's retry of a verification mask re-ran the whole agent under `NativeHarness` (and `HarborAgentHarness` in place), hidden by deterministic oracles: a policy whose answer hung the tests escaped its 0. *Grade-only retries:* `RetryRule.allows(..., regrade=)` refuses a verification-stage retry unless the attempt persisted its work (`HarnessResult.regradable`: a stored `artifacts/model.patch`, or a native separate verifier's quiesced hand-off in `verifier/handoff_ref.json` with the spec, store root, trace length and episode `verifier_world` needs); otherwise the attempt ends terminal-masked. `NativeHarness.regrade` (grade-only retries and `Runner.regrade`) rebuilds only what `verifier_world` reads from that ref (no task World is opened, nothing of the policy's starts) and grades a fresh verifier World; a ref it cannot read (`handoff_unreadable`) or whose spec is not the snapshot's (`handoff_spec_mismatch`) is a harness mask, never retried. In place (native, Harbor's installed-agent harness) the World is gone after the attempt, so the grade is retried inside it before it closes (`runtime/grading.py` `grade_in_place`: `grade_retries`, default 1, under one optional `scoring_budget_s` (`None`: each try is bounded only by the grader's own timeout) whose expiry during the first grade is `verifier_timeout` and during a later grade leaves the earlier try's failure standing, its reason noting the budget; try `i` grades into `<attempt>/regrade<i>/grade`, every report is kept as `verifier/report.r<i>.json` and `verifier/grades.json` lists the tries); Harbor in place gets one quiesce receipt per try and lists what an earlier clean grade left running as `leftovers` (killed, never flagged `policy_process_killed`); R-2's quiesce verdicts are unchanged, and none of its masks is retried by an agent re-run any more. On the regradable paths a failure the answer can cause (`grading.answer_sensitive`: a verifier timeout, a checker outage `check_infra`, a non-finite reward) is graded again at once inside the attempt and, when it repeats, decided there (no longer regradable), so `RetryRule(max_attempts=1)` cannot let it escape; a contaminated node or a transport failure still waits for a grade-only retry. A non-finite reward is `verifier_error` / `non_finite_reward` (it was a harness crash, retried as an agent run), and a `TimeoutError` a grader raises is `verifier_timeout`, not `boundary()`'s transport timeout. A multi-step step is graded again in place like a single-step grade, unless its task declares `step_grades_once` (`HarborMultiStepTask`: R-2's per-step state is used up by the step's grade), whose steps are graded once and never controlled. The journal marks a grade-only retry `grade_only` on its `retry_scheduled` and `dispatched` events. *The control:* a grade failure no further grade of this work will answer goes through `grading.settle`, inside the attempt and whatever `RetryRule` allows (so the R3 gate's `max_attempts=1` cannot let a hang escape). An answer-sensitive failure that repeated on the same work is decided by the task's control, the optional `RunTask.grade_control(workdir)` hook, graded by the same grader under its own budget (`control_timeout_s`, default 1800 s, raised to the task's own `control_timeout_s` when it declares a longer one, `grading.control_budget`: `HarborDirTask` its verifier timeout + 300 s, `HarborInPlaceTask` its agent and verifier timeouts plus margins): control passes → `crucible:policy_error` / `grader_failed_on_answer`, policy blame, scored 0, not retried; control fails, raises or times out → `verifier_error` with the original detail, task blame, masked, not retried; no control → the failure masked, not retried, `evidence.flags = ["no_control"]`. A control passes when the grader ran cleanly on it (a report, not masked, finite reward and outcome, no `meta.failure`), not when the oracle scores 1.0 (an optional `control_min_outcome` on the task sets a floor). Before the control, the policy's World is probed (`grading.liveness`, the task's `infra_cause`: a `dead` probe skips the control) and closed. Hooks: `SingleTurnTask` (`control_answer`, else the gold answer for the `full` parser: a judge that fails only on a prompt-injection answer scores it 0), toy `FibTask` (`ORACLE_PATCH`), `HarborInPlaceTask` (`solution/solve.sh` by Harbor's `OracleAgent` in a fresh World, then the same guarded grade), `HarborPatchTask` through `HarborDirTask` (`solution/model.patch`, or `oracle_patch`); `HarborMultiStepTask` and every multi-step step have none; `FlakyGradeTask` injects into the control too. On the workspace path (the Harbor patch seam included) the control runs in the same `PerfGuard` slot as the grade. Each controlled failure's evidence carries `control_reward` / `control_outcome` or `control_error`. *Blame and evidence:* a masked episode's failure keeps the blame, mask, retryable, detail and delivery the episode recorded (`infra.episode_failure`, used by `_masked_episode` and the workspace harness), so an unclassified policy exception stays unknown blame: masked, never retried, counted by `Health.unknown` (it fell back to `agent_run_error`'s harness blame and was retried). `boundary()` tries its non-policy rules (a wrapped `StageFailure`, NORMALIZE names, `TimeoutError`, `ConnectionError`/`OSError`) on the exception and then on its `__cause__` chain, at most 8 links and cycle-safe (NeMo-RL `failures.py`); the policy-blaming unencodable-text rule looks at the raised exception only. Every masked `Failure` carries `evidence = {probe: alive|dead|unprobed, control: passed|failed|none, status}`: `_masked_episode` records what `infra_cause` said, and `Runner._evidence` marks anything else masked as unprobed and uncontrolled (keeping the keys a failure already carries). *Review round 1:* the Runner's `attempt_timeout_s` clock firing after the agent phase is recorded at the phase the harness was in (`Attempt.phase`, kept by the native, workspace, Harbor and A2A harnesses), regradable only when the work was persisted (`Attempt.regradable`), so it is a re-grade or a terminal mask, never an agent re-run; a grade that raises is classified as `boundary()` would (`infra.classify_exception`) and graded again in place under the same rule as a masked report (a transport failure of the grader), and the reports of earlier tries (and their quiesce flags) are kept when a later try raises; a masked report whose reason quotes checker or judge output (`reason_untrusted`, `SingleTurnTask`) is classified by its `failure_kind` alone, so an injection answer cannot steer it to `stale_image` or `node_contention` and escape the control; a scoring budget that ran out during the first grade carries `evidence.budget_s` / `tries`, is flagged `not_repeated`, and its control must grade within that budget (`control_elapsed_s`), else the control fails (a task mask, never the policy's 0); a patch-mode NaN or infinite reward (`reward.txt`, `reward.json`, or the kit's non-finite mask) is `non_finite_reward`, answer-sensitive; a `HarborDirTask` grade cancelled by the budget removes its container before the control runs; the verifier World's own start, restore and fingerprint check run under `boundary()` (a serial host lock or a slow App is `transport_timeout`, re-graded from the hand-off, never the answer's `verifier_timeout`); a resumed run looks past an attempt the process died in, and a grade-only retry that reached no verdict, to the record that decides (`grading.deciding_record`: native, workspace and the journal's `grade_only` flag); a hand-off whose trace is missing or shorter than its `log_len` is `handoff_unreadable`. The A2A harness grades through `grade_in_place` and `settle` too (a NaN reward was a harness crash, retried as an agent run). *Review round 2:* `RLMHarness` runs `NativeHarness` on the Runner's own `Attempt` (the policy swapped in place, restored after), so the phase and hand-off it records reach the attempt clock (a copy hid them: a clock firing during the grade re-sampled the policy). The Runner hands its clock to the harness (`Attempt.clock`, an `asyncio.Timeout`): the verification phase opens the grading's own window on it (`grading.grading_window`: the scoring budget defaults to the clock's length, and the clock moves past that budget, the control's budget and a margin), so a grade that hangs ends inside the attempt as `verifier_timeout` and is settled with the control, never cut short as an `attempt_timeout` mask; a multi-step task's next agent phase gets back only the time it had left (`grading.agent_clock`). The scoring budget counts only the grade (`grading.unbudgeted` holds it): the verifier World's start, restore and close, Harbor in place's liveness probe, R-2 quiesce, leftovers listing and grader-guard host checks, and `HarborDirTask`'s image inspect and host transfers never run it out, so infrastructure is never the answer's `verifier_timeout` (R-2's verdicts are never pre-empted). Review round 3: a hold gives its time back to the attempt clock once it ends, and the control opens its own window on the clock (`grading.control_window`; the workspace harness opens the grading window again once its `PerfGuard` slot is held), so a decided hang reaches its control; the grader guard's scan, whose length the answer sets, holds the budget at most `grading.HOLD_CAP_S` per grade; a later try cut by the budget's remainder is no grade of the answer (`budget_cut_s`, never controlled); a Harbor multi-step step's pending guard window is closed by the next step's setup, never judged across an agent phase. Only an answer-sensitive failure that repeated is controlled: `grade_in_place` stamps `evidence.tries` (the answer-sensitive grades of this work, `seen` counting a grade-only retry's predecessor, `grading.seen_before`), `grading.decided` settles in the attempt only two such grades or a budget expiry, a single one (`grade_retries=0`, or after a transport failure or a contaminated node) is masked `not_repeated` without a control in place and left regradable on a persisted patch or hand-off; `attempt.regradable` is cleared before an answer-sensitive failure is graded again (`on_again`). A Runner timeout after the agent phase with nothing persisted (a workspace collect or World close before the patch is stored; Harbor's patch seam now marks its collect phase too) is not retryable: terminal-masked, never an agent re-run. A separate-verifier multi-step step is graded again inside the attempt in a fresh verifier World (a contaminated node, a transport failure, a verifier World that did not start). Harbor in place: a Verifier that timed out or raised leaves its grade window to be judged (`after_grade`) and the guard's baseline moved past it by the next grade of the World (`HarborInPlaceTask._guard_window`), so the first grade's own installs (`~/.local/bin/uv`) are never the policy's tampering. Acceptance check A11 holds the R-3 contract (re-graded on the same work, one agent dispatch) for in-place and separate tasks alike.
- **Lab audit R-4 (2026-10-19):** durable runs: one writer per run directory, and a content identity that refuses drift. Before R-4 a second `Runner` on a run id moved the first's live attempts to `aborted/` and reused its ledger reservations, and the run identity was a hash of ids (`native:llm-tool@1` says nothing of `max_steps`; `RunSpec.sampling`, the retry rule, a grader and a patch were never hashed), so a configuration changed between two runs resumed as the same run: a partial run mixed both, a finished one dispatched nothing and the report stated the new configuration for the old results. *The lease:* `RunJournal` takes an exclusive non-blocking OS lock on `runs/<id>/run.lock` (`core/locks.py`: `flock` on POSIX, `msvcrt` byte 0 on Windows; stdlib only; never deleted) before it reads `run.json`, before the ledger opens and before any attempt index is chosen, writes `owner.json` (`{host, pid, process_started, started_at}`: evidence, not the lock) and holds it for the whole of `Runner.run` and `Runner.regrade`, released on every exit path. A second opener, in this process or another, raises `RunBusy` (a `ValueError`, so the CLI reports a user error) naming the owner and touches nothing; the OS drops a dead holder's lock, so there is no stale-lock reclaim (single host, no network filesystems). `bench run` and `run_bench` probe the lease first (`journal.assert_free`), so a second bench of the same name writes nothing beside the first (the controls run, the pack's validation episodes, `validity.json`). *Never-reused indices:* `next_attempt` counts live `a<N>`, `aborted/a<N>` and `aborted/a<N>~<k>`, and a reservation the budget refuses keeps its index (`a<N>/refused.json`, written instead of removing the directory); it is no attempt, is not retried as one (`RunJournal.dispatched` is what the `RetryRule` counts) and is never moved; a record-less directory moves to `aborted/` without nesting. `BudgetLedger.reserve` is a plain `INSERT`: a duplicate op raises `OpReused`, which the Runner turns into a `ledger:` stop (a refused attempt) so nothing escapes `gather` while the lease is released; an exception that does escape one attempt cancels the others and waits for them before the lease is released, and a forked child closes its copy of the lock descriptor (`flock` is shared by a fork), so a surviving child cannot keep a dead holder's run locked. A run's summary counts the plan's own samples only (`RunSummary.superseded`: rows an accepted harness drift left under the old keys). *Identity v2* (`runtime/identity.py`): `run.json` keeps the id lists bench reads (`spec`) and stores a resolved document (`resolved`): every task, policy and harness described as JSON (`describe`: what the object declares through `identity_config()` (task content digests: a Harbor task tree, a pack tree, grader files, declared image refs; a policy's `PolicyRef(config=...)`; a harness's configuration, patch-map content and grading policy), or the plain data it holds, or, for a stateless object, its type), the retry rule, the sampling and the rollout mode. Fail closed: an object that cannot be inspected (a closure, a callable, an object with state and no declaration, a `PolicyRef` without `config`) raises `Undeclared` naming its dotpath before the run directory is touched. A resume compares every leaf but an operational denylist (`identity.DENY`: concurrency, budgets, wall clock, timeouts, backoff, health thresholds, docker and GPU hosts, sandbox ids, paths; matched below the id of a task, policy or harness, so an id such as `cleanup_build_dir` never drops its configuration; an endpoint (a URL, a host) is compared where it names the grader, model, agent or hardware measured; digest maps under `files` / `patches` are never filtered; a value under a credential-named key (`api_key`, `HF_TOKEN`) is stored as `<secret>` and so never compared or written, and other credential-shaped text is stored as a keyed digest, so an edited prompt is still a changed document) and refuses a difference with `IdentityDrift` listing `dotpath: old -> new` (`harnesses.native:llm-tool@1.limits.max_steps: 30 -> 100`); `RunSpec.accept_drift` (`bench run --accept-drift <dotpath>`) accepts named paths and opens a new identity epoch instead; a `run.json` written before v2 is refused unless `*` is accepted. Code versions (crucible version, git sha and dirty flag, Harbor and verifiers versions) are recorded per epoch and a change opens an epoch but never refuses; the one exception is `HarborAgentHarness`, whose id digests the Harbor version, so it declares `harbor_version` as a compared leaf (an upgrade would otherwise run every sample again, paid, with no refusal); records of an epoch after the first carry `meta.epoch`, and a bench report whose terminal samples span more than one epoch splits its counts and means by epoch and warns that the headline metrics mix them. The ids of the in-tree harnesses carry a 12-hex digest of what produces the work (`name@version#<digest>`: limits, limit policy, `checkpoint_every`, collect mode, the patch map's content; the A2A harness's agent, config, deadline and dialect); the grading policy (R-3) stays out of the id and in `identity_config`. Existing tests were updated for the strictness: every `PolicyRef` declares a `config`, every fake that holds state declares `identity_config()` (never counters or recorded lists), and a test that builds a harness id takes it from the harness. Patterns: NeMo-RL `rollout_checkpoint.py` (fail-closed fingerprint, dotpath diff), Harbor `lock.json` (task digests compared, its own version recorded), Repo2RLEnv `run.json` and `lock_file` (PORT).
- **Lab audit R-5 (2026-10-19), retry backoff (item 3):** a retry no longer waits an unjittered, unbounded `backoff * 2**(n-1)`: every sample an outage failed together came back together, and a provider's `Retry-After` was ignored by the Runner. `RetryRule.backoff(failure, tries, base_s)` is the capped full-jitter curve of verifiers `v1/utils/retries.py:40-42` (the one `actors/llm._retry_delay` already used), never shorter than the failure's `retry_after` (capped at `retry_after_cap_s`, 300 s) and otherwise never longer than `backoff_cap_s` (30 s); `retry_backoff_s=0` keeps immediate retries unless a Retry-After was given. The new `Failure.retry_after` (seconds, None when unknown, dropped from canonical JSON while None) is filled from the capture proxy's last row (`agent_evidence._upstream_failure`) and from the native client (`LLMError.retry_after`, set from the last answer's header, carried by `LLMToolPolicy` and `run_episode` to `episode_failure`). The two caps are operational (`identity.DENY`), so the run fingerprint and every existing resume are unchanged.
- **Lab audit R-5 (2026-10-19), host calls (item 4):** no host call an attempt makes blocks the event loop. Before R-5 the collect hook's git (`verify/collect.py`, `runtime/collect.py`, `workspace._base_sha`), `GpuHost.put/get/path` (rsync with no timeout, ssh) and `HarborDirTask`'s docker calls were synchronous `subprocess.run`s (or a thread the cancelled grade left running), so one stalled call froze every attempt's agent loop, every `wait_for` and the wall clock. Now every git, docker, rsync and ssh call goes through `world.exec._run_subprocess` (a thread-pool `Popen` in its own session, never an asyncio subprocess; a timeout or a cancel kills the whole session); `_run_subprocess_raw` is the same without the text decoding, for a binary stdout (`HarborBackend.docker` returns the grader guard's `docker cp ... -` tar streams as the bytes they are). *Collect:* `collect`, `changed_paths`, `tree_changed`, `repo_broken` and `_base_sha` are coroutines; each git call is bounded by `verify.collect.GIT_TIMEOUT_S` (300 s). A call past its timeout is infrastructure, never the agent's outcome: a timed-out `git status` counts as "changed" (never "the agent did nothing", scored 0), a timed-out collection is an empty patch with an error naming the call (never a half-built index's patch, which would be graded as the answer) and the killed git's `index.lock` is removed, so it ends `crucible:empty_collection` (masked, re-collected from the preserved workspace), and `repo_broken` reports only a git that answered with a failure. *Hosts:* `GpuHost` gets `aexec`, `apath`, `aput`, `aget` (the sync `put`, `get`, `path` are gone; the sync `exec` stays for `PerfGuard`'s threaded probe only); a call past its timeout (`TRANSFER_TIMEOUT_S` 1800 s for rsync) raises `HostTimeout`, a `ConnectionError` that is not a `TimeoutError`, so it is `transport_unreachable` (regradable) and never the answer's `verifier_timeout`. *`HarborDirTask`:* `docker run` is awaited (its own timeout is still `verifier_timeout`; an inspect or `rm` that does not answer is a `HostTimeout`); a cancelled or timed-out grade runs `docker rm -f <name>` (shielded from a second cancel), and the host staging is deleted (`rm -rf` of the absolute `apath`, no longer the cwd-relative path that missed the local host's `$HOME`) only once the container is known gone (`docker run` returned, or `rm -f` returned 0 or "No such container"; a cancel during a transfer, before any container, deletes it at once); otherwise it stays and `<gdir>/staging_kept.json` names the host, path and container (evidence for the sandbox sweep). OpenEnv client mode's Docker start (`ClientEnv.astart`) is awaited too. Harbor's own `env.exec` calls are its async API and unchanged.
- **Lab audit R-5 (2026-10-19), breakers (item 1):** an outage pauses the run instead of masking its samples. Before R-5 a provider that returned 503s for ten minutes made every sample burn its three attempts and end terminal-masked, and the mask-fraction rule then stopped the run: it measured the outage, and what the failing attempts had spent was gone. `runtime/breaker.py` keeps one breaker per provider endpoint (`PolicyRef.endpoint`, else the policy id), per host (`breaker_host` or `host` declared by the task or harness, else `local`) and per (task, harness). A masked failure of infra, harness or task blame counts against the keys its kind names (`counted`: a provider kind the provider; a transport, session or sandbox kind the host and the task; anything else the task, including a grader that fails its own control, `Failure.evidence.control`); policy and unknown blame and `crucible:credits_exhausted` count against none. A key opens after `BreakerRule.failures` (3) consecutive failing attempts (a host key: across at least two tasks) or `task_samples` (3) consecutive failing samples (a sample's retries count once); a verdict, an attempt that was not masked, ends the run of failures. While a key is open its owed samples wait before dispatch (`Breakers.admit`, outside the concurrency slot: no index, no ledger reservation, no attempt dir), and an infra-masked attempt whose key is open is never terminal: it is journaled `deferred` (`meta.breaker` on its `record.json`), stays owed, and uses up none of its sample's retries (`RunJournal.tries`). A harness- or task-blamed failure counts toward opening a key but its own attempt follows `RetryRule` as before (deferring it would re-run the policy for as long as the key stays open: P23), as does a failure no retry could change. After `cooldown_s` (30 s; doubling per failed probe, never shorter than the failure's Retry-After, never longer than a quarter of `quarantine_s`) one probe attempt runs and its verdict closes the key (only the claimed probe's, or that of an attempt admitted after the key opened: a verdict of an attempt already in flight when the outage began says nothing of it). A key whose probe still fails after `quarantine_s` (900 s) is quarantined: journaled with its reason, its samples left owed (`RunSummary.quarantined`, `quarantined_samples`; bench `run_info`), and the rest of the run continues; `RunSummary.stopped` is not set. State is in memory and starts closed on every `Runner.run` (a resume probes again); the transitions are journal events (`waiting`, `breaker_open`, `breaker_probe`, `breaker_closed`, `deferred`, `quarantine`, `quarantined`). `RunSpec.breaker=None` restores the earlier behaviour; the settings are operational (`identity.DENY`). Samples that wait also see the stop rules and the wall clock.
- **Lab audit R-5 (2026-10-19), resume (item 2):** a resume recovers what an outage cost. Before R-5 a sample the outage left masked-terminal at the admission, seed or agent stage was done for good (`Runner.regrade` covers verification only), and the resume seeded `Health` with those rows, so a run whose every earlier sample was masked stopped on the mask-fraction rule at its first dispatch. `Runner._reopens` selects the terminal rows that are masked, infra-blamed, retryable, not in `NON_RETRYABLE_KINDS`, not `crucible:credits_exhausted`, at stage admission, seed or agent; the resume dispatches them again (the `resume` event lists them under `reopened`, `done` counts the rest) with one fresh retry budget: the `RetryRule` counts their attempts after the terminal one (`RunJournal.tries(..., since=<its index>)`), and an owed sample counts from 0 as before. A verification or collect mask is never reopened (the policy is not sampled again because a grade failed: R-3), nor a harness-, task- or unknown-blamed one (not an outage), nor a policy's scored 0. A reopened sample that ends the call deferred, quarantined or stopped keeps its older masked row as its newest (`journal.terminal`: the newest row wins), so the next resume reopens it again, with another fresh budget. `Health` is no longer seeded: the stop rules count only the samples that end in this `Runner.run` call, so a resume after an outage starts its mask fraction from zero; `RunSummary.by_failure` is computed from the plan's final records, so it still reports every masked kind.
- **Lab audit R-6 (2026-10-19): sandbox hygiene.** Every container Crucible starts now names its owner and an expiry, ends by itself at that expiry, is removed on every exit path of the code that started it, and is swept after a crash. *What was wrong:* a World sandbox was `docker run -d --rm --init ... sleep infinity` with two labels (`crucible.world`, `crucible.sandbox`), so a runner that was killed or OOM-killed left its containers (GPU ones included) running for ever and nothing swept them; every `RunTask.open` that failed after `World.start` leaked the World, and `HealthcheckError` / `ResetHookError` are retryable infra, so each retry leaked one more container (the harness's `world` is still None, so it closed nothing); `World.close` was unshielded and set `_closed` first, and callers swallowed close errors; a cancelled `HarborDirTask` grade could leave its container (a daemon can create the container after its client was killed, after the first `rm -f` answered "No such container"); `close_pools` had no caller; and PerfGuard counted every running `^crucible` container as contention, so one orphan from a crashed run held gate R1 (which waits up to 12 h) for 12 h. *What changed* (`world/lifecycle.py`, the RLM kernel's own lifecycle moved to the world layer and shared: one design in the repo; the kernel imports `LIFE_LOOP`, `CTL_DIR`, `RENEW_SLACK_S`, `DEFAULT_LIFETIME_S`, `lifetime_for` and `finish` back under its old names, and its `crucible.rlm.*` labels and reapers are unchanged): (1) *labels* on every World sandbox, pooled container and grade container: `crucible.owner=<host>:<pid>:<process start>` and `crucible.expires=<epoch>`, plus, inside a Runner attempt, `crucible.run`, `crucible.attempt` (`<sample>-a<N>`) and `crucible.lease` (the absolute path of the run's R-4 `run.lock`: the sweep finds the owning run's lock by it); `crucible.renewable=1` marks a keeper someone renews (its expiry label is a floor, not a promise). (2) *A bounded life:* `sleep infinity` is gone. Outside a Runner attempt the keeper is `sleep <ttl>` (6 h: `Hygiene.default_s`); inside an attempt, and in the warm pools, it is the kernel's renewable life loop (`sh -c LIFE_LOOP`, a root-only `/crucible-ctl` tmpfs holding the deadline), renewed while the attempt scope lives (R-3's grading window can move the attempt clock past `attempt_timeout_s`, and a fixed life would end an in-place grade or its control); the TTL is `lifetime_for(attempt_timeout_s)`, the attempt backstop plus `Hygiene.margin_s` (900 s, at least `floor_s` 600 s; 6 h without a backstop); `--rm` stays, so a container whose keeper ends removes itself. Grade containers carry the labels but no in-container TTL (the grade command is the task's own): a hung grade ends when its `docker run` timeout kills the CLI and its `rm -f` runs, or, after a runner crash, at the next sweep through its `crucible.expires`. (3) *Every exit path:* `DockerSandbox.start` records the container in the scope before `docker run`, shields the run, and on a cancel or a failure removes what it made (a run that finishes after `START_GRACE_S` is removed by a done-callback); `DockerSandbox.close` and `World.close` are shielded, idempotent (a second caller awaits the first), bounded (`CLOSE_TIMEOUT_S`, `World.close_timeout_s`) and rc-checked (`rm -f` succeeds on rc 0 or "No such container"), and they *record* a failure in the attempt's scope instead of raising a new one (a verifier World closes in a `finally` after its grade: a failed removal must not turn a computed grade into a lost one; the attempt's reclaim retries it and `teardown.json` reports it); `lifecycle.opening(world)` is the async context every `RunTask.open` uses (control, single-turn, EOG, NeMo-Gym, AWM, OpenEnv import, long-context and the in-place Harbor task), so an `open()` that fails after `start()` closes what it started, and for any other `open()` the attempt scope's reclaim closes the Worlds and removes the containers its attempt started, by name and by `crucible.attempt` label; every dispatched attempt (a grade-only retry, a cancelled one, a `Runner.regrade` attempt) writes `attempt/teardown.json` `{terminated, failed, still_up, released}` before `record.json` (a refused reservation is no attempt and has none), and a failed or still-up container is also a `teardown` event; `HarborDirTask` labels and records its grade container before `docker run`, looks again `RECHECK_S` after a cancelled or failed run, and removes in a shielded `finally` before the staging goes (or writes `staging_kept.json`); `close_pools(run)` runs at the end of `Runner.run` and `regrade` before the lease is released (a `pools_closed` event), and an atexit reaper removes the process's own `crucible.owner` containers at interpreter exit (SIGKILL bypasses atexit: the TTL and the sweep cover it). Pooled containers belong to the run, never to an attempt (built with `Scope.run_scope()`, keyed by (run id, signature), kept for twice the ttl, renewed on hand-out and by the borrowing attempt's renewer; a container whose renewal fails is dead and replaced); the pool's hygiene spares exactly the keeper, identified as the earliest-started child of PID 1 with a keeper form (`sleep ` or `sh -c `) and its descendants (a policy-planted `sleep 999` starts later and is killed). (4) *The sweep* (`Runner._sweep`, a `sweep` event; `RunSpec.hygiene.sweep`) runs at the start of `run` and `regrade`, after the run's lease is taken (a resume is just another start), over the local daemon and every docker host the run used before (`RunJournal.sandbox_hosts`), and removes: a container carrying this run's own lease path (`own_run`); a same-host container whose run's `run.lock` is free or gone (`lease_free`; held means live, even past its label); and any other `crucible.owner` container past `crucible.expires`. It decides by R-4's lock, never by pid (`os.kill(pid, 0)` has no meaning on Windows), probes only an absolute path named `run.lock` and never creates a lock file, never touches a container without `crucible.owner` (RLM kernels, Harbor compose containers, the gVisor toolbox), and never fails the run. PerfGuard ignores a container past its expiry unless it is a renewable keeper (a running renewable container always counts). `RunSpec.hygiene` (`Hygiene`: `margin_s`, `floor_s`, `default_s`, `sweep`) is operational (`identity.DENY`): changing it never drifts a run, and the fingerprints of existing runs are unchanged. *Not covered (recorded under Remaining):* Harbor-managed containers, App and import containers, cloud sandboxes. Patterns (no code copied): mini-swe-agent `sleep 2h` (MIT), NeMo-Gym label plus `sleep ttl_s` (Apache-2.0), verifiers register-before-start, shielded idempotent close and atexit (MIT), agentenv `still_up` (Apache-2.0), karotte run-keyed cleanup and lock-probed liveness (MIT); the design reused is the RLM kernel's. The unit tests (`tests/unit/test_m10_lab_audit_r6_*.py`) and the Docker acceptance suite (`tests/redteam/test_m10_lab_audit_r6_docker.py`: kill -9 then TTL and sweep, an `open()` that fails three times, a cancel during start and during a grade, a pooled run) are written but not yet run.

## Observers and progress (2026-10-20, M7 R7)

An episode observer (`verify.progress.ProgressTracker`, a checkpoint sidecar) measures the episode and never ends it:
`run_episode` switches off an observer whose `on_start`, `on_step` or `on_end` raises and records it in
`EpisodeResult.observer_failures` (its `observed` entry is `{"failed": <error>}`). A subgoal query fault used to escape
as `harness:RuntimeError` (M12) or `agent_run_error` (native), masked with unknown blame, and the policy could cause it.
The native and workspace harnesses and `train.world_env` grade the episode and record
`meta.progress = {"progress_unavailable": <error>}`; the partial stream is kept as `progress.failed.jsonl`, so the
record has no per-turn ΔΦ (M6 R3 trains it outcome-only or refuses the batch). It is not a rollout flag: flags mean
hack suspicion (`studio/datasets.py`, the hack judge). Per-call ΔΦ joins rows to calls by `tool_call_id`
(`runtime.record.credit_from_progress`). The workspace harness takes a grade's `meta.flags` and policy verdict
(`_take_findings`) as the native harness does.
