**Five cross-paper takeaways for Part 2**

1. **Condition the proposer on failures and near neighbours, then gate on "interestingly new", not only on learnability.** OMNI-EPIC learned 16 tasks with nearest successes and failures in context, 7 without failures, 5 with random examples, and 0 when the prompt dropped the learnability notion. OMNI's model of interestingness matched an oracle and beat learning progress alone (Crafter: 82 vs 55 tasks learned). Daedalus feeds the Explorer the accepted tasks, a live tag tally and a guideline bank distilled from too-easy/too-hard refinements. Rainbow Teaming picks the target cell first and mutates toward it. For F3/F11: 5 nearest kept + 5 nearest failed tasks with reasons, an explicit band statement for the named student, a different-family interestingness check against the 10 nearest, and ANNECS-OMNI (novel, in band at birth, later solved, judged interesting) as the factory's progress curve.
2. **Every LLM-judged reward in this batch was hacked or decoupled at the top. Execution and pairwise checks are what held.** ARE's soft judge was beaten by templated pseudo-code stuffed into the final message; a task-agnostic sanity check fixed it. Qwen-AgentWorld's rubric reward drew self-praise; a 10% rule-based anchor, content-type matching and keeping the reasoning out of the judge's view fixed it. QDAIF's quality score decouples from humans above 0.995. Rainbow's score-based updates gamed Llama Guard and stopped improving, while pairwise judging with position swaps kept refining. ARE's oracle-write-DAG verifier scored 0.98 agreement against 0.72 for an in-context judge on 450 labelled trajectories. WEFT uses disagreement between rubric and executable scores as an RL admission filter (1,954 of 5,000 tasks kept). EnvACE and Agent0 train on self-generated observations or majority-vote labels with no external check, which is a pattern to avoid.
3. **Trace-based failure analysis turns into targeted tasks with measurable rules.** TRACE keeps a skill only if its contrastive gap (lacking-rate on failures minus on successes) is at least 0.20 and its failure coverage at least 0.10, stable over repeated labellings. It then builds one seeded, single-skill env per skill and gains +15.3 on τ² and +15 on SWE-bench Verified, far more than prompting the same skills (GEPA). WEFT routes each failure to the policy, World, task or verifier, then revalidates. In three rounds tool errors fell 45% and benchmarks rose 3.7–5.3 points. For traces to tasks: relabel per sub-trajectory (Learn-by-interact, up to +14), strip the trace's CoT and minimally edit toward what the state diff shows (AgentBrew), and re-execute the lifted goal forward (OS-Genesis). In Crucible each relabel must then be rebuilt as a snapshot plus a goal diff and pass F4–F6.
4. **Hardness should come from structure and controlled perturbation, with knob settings constant within a GRPO group.** Daedalus gets hardness from scope words (all, latest, past page 1) and eases by shrinking the sweep, never by revealing ids. SearchEyes lost 7.7 points without anti-shortcut filters and gained from disambiguating constraint edges and name concealment. Magnet uses merge, insert and split operations to plant nested calls, long dependencies and missing information. In Qwen-AgentWorld, uncontrolled simulated RL gave no gain, while controlled perturbations (pagination, withheld intermediates, injected errors) gave +12.3 on MCPMark. In NoisyAgent, noise on every rollout was worse than no noise, while mixed clean/noisy subgroups with separate normalisation and a gap-triggered schedule helped. FEEs need the same enrichment for every rollout in a group, and their scaffolding eroded clarification skills. RandomWorld's dead-step trimming and privileged-solver informativeness check make "solution first" executable.
5. **Measure diversity by behaviour and by how tasks rank solvers, and watch for template locking.** Enhanced POET's PATA-EC describes a task by the clipped, rank-normalised scores of a solver population, and its ANNECS metric counts tasks that are new, in band and solved. WEFT drops tasks whose tool chain is a subsequence of another's and tracks per-App tool coverage. The ACE survey asks for diversity measured only on valid, in-band items, plus joint slices to catch template locking. In Persona Hub, constraints raise output similarity, so personas alone do not certify diversity inside a cell. Daedalus's Surveyor turns executed probes into a weighted axis plus a tag manifest with target shares, which is a cheap DomainSpec bootstrap.

**Caution across the batch:** TRACE (τ² discovery rollouts on the test tasks), NoisyAgent (trains on τ² and VitaBench domains it evaluates), Magnet (BFCL-v3 implementations in its pool) and Learn-by-interact (SWE-bench Verified files as documents) all leak benchmark material into synthesis. For Crucible this reinforces the sealed-split rule: nothing from benchmark tasks may feed F0–F3 or the skill selector.

## 2610.08048 — Daedalus: Bootstrapping Agent Memory from Self-Generated Tasks (Edy et al., 2026; preprint, Illuin Technology)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–G incl. prompts).  Relevance to Part 2: high. It is not RL (memory, no weight updates), but its Explorer/Solver calibration loop, Surveyor coverage manifest, and "failure-then-streak" acceptance rule are directly reusable for F3, F9 and the difficulty controller; it also measures an LLM judge against official verifiers and shows self-generated tasks rank models like a real test set.
**Problem.** Agents in a new environment have no curated tasks and no oracle verifier, so procedural memory cannot be built the usual way. Daedalus generates calibrated practice tasks itself and mines validated heuristics from solver failures.
**Method / algorithm.** Agents: Surveyor, Explorer, Solver, Judge, Extractor, Consolidator. Auxiliary agents use the larger model of a family (GPT-5.4 high), Solver and test agent the smaller (GPT-5.4-mini).
1. *Surveyor (once).* Explores the live env read-only and writes (a) a coverage goal: chosen axis (app/area/task shape), a weighted distribution justified by real use, not endpoint count, generator guidance and pitfalls; (b) a closed **tag manifest**: each tag has a definition and `target_share` in (0,1]. A tag must be able to be false, so anything with target above ~0.5 is a baseline requirement, not a tag. The surveyor may only report after answering five probes per area from things it ran: record contents and cross-area keys; what is past page 1; behaviour on wrong and stale ids; what a write does to related records, read back; what it refuses and with which error.
2. *Explorer session (n sessions, 5 concurrent, 40-turn cap).* Sees the survey, the live UNDER/OK/OVER tag tally, the list of already-accepted tasks ("do something different") and the evolving guideline bank. It explores, writes one task and completes it itself (feasibility). Output: a short natural request; the minimal tool-call solution; **success conditions on the end state, as changes from the start state, path-agnostic**. Doctrine: under-specify on purpose (delete every id/amount/date the solver could find); hardness from *scope* (superlatives, universals, relative time windows, pagination, none/many matches, policy limits), not from more steps or more apps.
3. *Solver loop (Alg. 3).* Reset env; solver attempts with current heuristic h; Judge checks trajectory against conditions. On failure the Extractor rewrites h from the instruction and failed trajectory **without seeing which conditions failed** (pushes general lessons). Stop on N_s=3 consecutive successes or N_f=8 total failures. Outcomes: accepted (≥1 failure then a 3-streak), too_easy (never failed), too_hard (8 failures).
4. *Refinement.* If too_easy/too_hard, Explorer.Refine makes a small targeted change in the same spirit, up to N_r=5 times; the easier direction must reduce scope or remove one requirement, never name records or spell steps.
5. *Guideline bank.* After any session with >1 variant, an LLM updates a compact list of general task-design principles from the progression of variants and their solver traces (add/modify/delete; never entity-specific).
6. *Consolidation.* One LLM call merges accepted heuristics into atomic, concrete, deduplicated items (JSON with topic tags); injected whole at task start.
**Verification / grading.** LLM judge over trajectory vs self-written end-state conditions; told to trust effects not narration, and that a group outcome ("every…") is UNMET unless the trajectory proves the group size (anti-pagination hack). Measured against official verifiers: AppWorld P .943 / R .846 / κ .807; τ²-retail κ .749; AutomationBench κ .729; inter-judge κ .85–1.0. Precision > recall (rejects true successes more than it accepts failures). Hackable: the judge reads the trajectory, so a solver that prints convincing tool output could fool it; no state check. They removed τ² tasks whose reference solution exchanged >1 item per call because the env grades argument order (a real verifier bug found by construction).
**Scale & cost.** 90 sessions per benchmark; acceptance 97.8% of sessions on AppWorld; 118 refinements; generation $109.7 (AppWorld); 1,225 solver rollouts (13.6/task; 520 on first versions, 705 on refined ones) vs 592 for the curated variant. Most cost is Explorer + Judge, not Solver.
**Key results.** MSR gain over no memory: +15.9 (AppWorld 44.3→60.2), +10.0 (τ² retail), +4.3 (AutomationBench); pass^5 ×1.7–2.2. Within error of best training-task method in 4/6 columns. Cumulative ablation: single explorer trajectory *hurts* (35.7 < 44.3); adding Solver traces +15.8; repeated-success validation +4.5; guideline bank −12% cost; survey cuts total cost 48% vs (d) and raises acceptance 86.7→97.8%. Counterfactual replay on 81 accepted tasks: heuristic in context raises MSR 66.7→77.0 (paired bootstrap CI [2.1, 18.5]). Generated tasks rank 9 models like the AppWorld test set (Kendall τ=0.89, 34/36 pairs). Cross-family banks help every base agent (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B +29.3 with its own family's bank). Beyond 90 sessions MSR falls (bank 140 sessions: 53.7) because consolidation misstates heuristic scope.
**Failure modes & limits.** LLM judge only (κ 0.73–0.81); no dual-control envs; frozen bank; consolidation at scale loses scope; an early near-duplicate filter on expected tool paths was used once and is not part of the method. Critique: calibration target is "fails at least once then passes 3 in a row", which is a recoverable-failure band, not a p̂ band; tasks are calibrated to one solver; judged success conditions are written by the same Explorer that knows the solution.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Surveyor must answer fixed probes per area from executed calls before it may write the coverage plan.
- Tag manifest with target shares, live UNDER/OK/OVER tally fed back to the proposer.
- Tags with target share >0.5 are requirements, not coverage axes.
- Hardness from scope words (all, latest, last N days, beyond page 1), not from chaining apps.
- When easing a too-hard task, shrink the sweep, never reveal ids or steps.
- Success conditions as end-state deltas, path-agnostic, no incidental details.
- Judge rule: group outcomes need evidence of the whole group.
- Hide the failed-condition list from the lesson writer to avoid task-specific fixes.
- A streak of N consecutive successes is a cheap guard against lucky passes.
- Keep a compact, curated "difficulty guideline" playbook evolved from variant progressions.
- Self-generated, calibrated tasks are a usable model-ranking proxy when no test set exists.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F0/F3: adopt the Surveyor (executed probes → axis + weighted distribution + tag manifest with target shares) as the bootstrap of a `DomainSpec.axes` for a new World, and the live tag tally as the F3 quota signal (complements MAP-Elites cell fill). F3/F4: adopt the Explorer doctrine (under-specification; scope-based hardness; end-state-delta conditions) as the proposer prompt core, but keep F4 executed oracle + F5 code checks instead of an LLM judge. F9: the too_easy/too_hard/refine loop is a cheap difficulty controller; adapt its outcome to p̂ bands from k rollouts and require that a refinement changes the behaviour signature. §3.8: the guideline bank is a low-risk generator-loop artifact (prompt-side, versioned). F10: "failure then N-streak with the lesson" is a usable lucky-pass guard. Avoid: trajectory-reading LLM judge as the grader. Interface: `SurveyReport{axis, distribution, tags:[{tag, definition, target_share}]}` → DomainSpec draft; `RefineRequest{task, direction, history}`. Priority P1 (Surveyor + tag tally P0-cheap for world_synth domains).

## 2609.36887 — WEFT: Scaling Tool-Use Post-Training for General-Purpose Agents (Mao et al., 2026; preprint, ECNU/Fudan)
Read: FULL (PDF; main text and App. A–D).  Relevance to Part 2: high. A full World→task→verifier→trace-attribution→revision loop at 8k-MCP scale, with execution-driven component repair (environment vs task vs verifier vs policy) and a verifier-consistency filter before RL.
**Problem.** Scaling executable environments alone does not give reliable reward: failures come from broken tools, infeasible tasks and wrong verifiers, and attributing them to the policy trains on noise. WEFT scales the whole "agentic interaction system" (environment, task, harness, evaluator) and repairs it from its own rollouts.
**Method / algorithm.**
1. *Stateful environment synthesis.* MCP tool definitions from public docs and business requirements. An env-synthesis agent infers shared entities, read/write dependencies and state transitions over the whole tool set; instantiates a shared DB schema; writes per-tool contracts (argument→state mapping, reads, writes, returns); generates a FastAPI backend. Workspace state binds to isolated file runtimes. Validation: interface names and arg schemas; generated normal and boundary calls; queries checked against state, updates against DB/workspace diff and read-back. Error type routes the repair: structural→schema, mapping→contract, runtime/state access→backend.
2. *Certified atomic tasks (Alg. 1).* Per MCP, a task agent writes atomic tasks (one intent, bounded tool calls) plus E2E tests. Keep a task only if executing from an independent initial state satisfies its completion conditions on returns and persistent state. **Tool-chain orthogonality:** drop a task whose ordered tool-name chain is a (non-contiguous) subsequence of another's; identical chains keep the earlier. **Tool coverage** = distinct tools in retained chains / MCP tools; iterate (failures, redundancy, uncovered tools fed back) until coverage ≥ η or round budget R.
3. *Coverage-aware cross-MCP composition.* Five difficulty profiles (Simple…Expert) give ranges of atomic-task count, MCP count and domain breadth plus a corpus share; largest-remainder allocation turns shares into quotas. Sampler favours under-used domains/MCPs, ranks combos by shared entities, complementary ops, low redundancy; requires capacity surplus Σc_m ≥ ⌈ρk⌉, ρ>1; an LLM checks the MCP set supports a coherent business objective.
4. *Grounded task graph.* A composition agent links atomic tasks into a DAG (nodes: intent, inputs, outcome; edges: information/state dependencies; entity bindings across MCPs; given vs retrieved info). Deterministic checks: nodes use selected tasks, bindings conflict-free, every input comes from initial state or an upstream outcome.
5. *Progressive state grounding* in 3 rounds: target entities + minimal executable state; cross-tool relations/constraints; history and distractors. Completion conditions preserved; schema + reachability checks; snapshot as resettable s0.
6. *Views and harnesses.* Same objective/state/conditions under Agentic (full brief) or SimUser (persona-sampled user discloses requests in dependency order, one atomic task per turn), × vague/partial/full disclosure, × ReAct / OpenClaw / Hermes harnesses.
7. *Verifier synthesis.* Given completion conditions and a reference execution accepted by a semantic LLM, an agent writes deterministic checks over state diffs and tool returns, plus a rubric; checks tested on reference evidence. Reward R(τ)=mean over K checkpoints of r_k∈{0,1} (atomic tasks + overall goal).
8. *Execution-driven self-evolution.* Attribution agent reads graphs, traces, checkpoint outcomes, state evidence; cross-view and cross-harness patterns localise faults; **disagreement between rubric and executable scores flags the verifier**. Routing: env→backend, task/state→graph and s0, eval→verifier; policy failures leave the task unchanged. Re-instantiate, rerun construction checks, fresh rollouts; 3 rounds.
9. *Training.* SFT by prefix-preserving rejection sampling (teacher Kimi K3, retry only the failed atomic turn from the restored snapshot, k=3). RL task filter: 16 pilot rollouts per task (Nex-N2-mini) scored by rubric LLM judge and executable verifier; keep tasks where both Pearson and Spearman pass thresholds (1,954/5,000). Atomic-turn GRPO: at each turn, G segments from identical restored state; A=(r−mean)/(sd+ε) within the group; one passing segment continues; turns flagged by deterministic checks (no-progress repetition, malformed calls, interface violations) get min(A,0).
10. *MegaMCP.* Shared MCP processes outside sandboxes, private COW DB + workspace per (rollout, MCP), content-addressed versioned registry, async debounced snapshots, generation fencing; the verifier runs in the serving layer on initial DB, final DB and full tool-call ledger, invisible to the agent.
**Verification / grading.** Deterministic checkpoints over state diffs and returns; rubric judge used only as a consistency audit, never as RL reward. Hack surface reduced by server-side verifier and hidden DB files; positive credit masked on degenerate turns. Residual: reference execution is accepted by an LLM; checks written against one reference may be too strict.
**Scale & cost.** 8,172 MCPs, 64,755 tools, 41,695 certified atomic tasks, 11,884 composed tasks (median 20 atomic turns; mix 27/36/15/10/12% across 5 profiles; >half multi-domain). RL: 1,954 of 5,000 kept (39%). No $ reported. MegaMCP: upload 773.5→34.8 MiB at 1,000 tasks; memory −77.6% to −95.7%; cold TTFT 10.5→4.8 s.
**Key results.** WEFT-14B beats Agent-World-14B by +6.41 BFCL V4, +2.23 τ², +12.27 Claw-Eval. WEFT-35B-A3B: Toolathlon-Verified 45.99, AutomationBench 26.50. Same tasks, mixed views beat either alone (+3.18 avg over Agentic); adding OpenClaw+Hermes harnesses +9.71 mean (+13.58 Toolathlon). Three self-evolution rounds: tool-error rate in selected trajectories 1.76%→0.96% and +3.65 to +5.25 pp downstream at fixed tasks and budget. Atomic-turn GRPO beats trajectory-level GRPO on τ² by +5.69 (8B) / +4.43 (14B); trajectory-level GRPO regressed τ² below SFT.
**Failure modes & limits.** No cost accounting; attribution agent's precision not measured; thresholds for the Pearson/Spearman filter not stated; no hackability audit; one teacher (Kimi K3) for all SFT. Critique: "policy failure ⇒ keep task" depends entirely on the attribution agent; no anchor set guards its drift.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Route repairs by error class: structural→schema, mapping→contract, runtime→backend.
- Drop tasks whose tool chain is a subsequence of another's (cheap behavioural dedup).
- Track per-App tool coverage of retained tasks; generate toward uncovered tools.
- Capacity surplus ρ>1 before composing tasks across Apps.
- Ground state in 3 passes: minimal, relations, distractors; re-check reachability.
- Same task, many disclosure views and harnesses = more training value without new tasks.
- Use rubric-vs-executable score disagreement as a verifier-defect detector and an RL admission filter.
- Snapshot per turn so retries and GRPO groups start from identical state.
- Mask only positive advantage on degenerate turns.
- Run the verifier in the tool-serving layer on start DB, end DB and call ledger.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F1: adopt contract-first App synthesis and error-class repair routing; G13 maps to their normal/boundary call tests. F3/F8: tool-chain subsequence dedup is a concrete F8 behavioural layer and per-App tool coverage a diversity meter. F2: 3-round state grounding fits SeedBundle (distractor_density as the last round). F5/F6: "rubric vs executable disagreement over k pilot rollouts" is a cheap verifier-defect gate (add as a G-check before F9; uses frontier pilot rollouts, not the student). F12/§3.8 World and verifier loops: the attribution→targeted-revision→revalidate→fresh-rollout loop is the template, but add our guards (anchor set, frozen expected states). Task views (agentic vs SimUser × disclosure) are a cheap knob family (observability/hints). Interface: `FailureAttribution{side: policy|world|task|verifier, evidence_refs, target_component}`. Priority P0 for the consistency filter and attribution routing; P1 for composition and views.

## 2609.32750 — CUA-Sandbox: Efficient Environments for Computer-Use Agent Reinforcement Learning (Yan et al., 2026; preprint, A*STAR/HKUST/NUS et al.)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–G).  Relevance to Part 2: low–medium. Pure rollout infrastructure; no task synthesis or verification. Useful for World snapshot/fork semantics (F2 seeds, G2 determinism, many tasks per snapshot) and for the CUA/Harbor tier of the World.
**Problem.** Each RL rollout on real software (WebArena, VisualWebArena, OSWorld) usually gets its own container with a fully initialised runtime; memory and init cost scale with concurrency. The paper argues mutable *state*, not the runtime, is the unit of environment independence.
**Method / algorithm.**
- Environment = ⟨shared runtime P, immutable base B, private capsule K_i, binding table Φ_i⟩ instead of ⟨private runtime P_i, B, S_i⟩.
- *Task resource contract* Γ_τ = {(key, state class, backend adapter, lifecycle policy)} built from task setup, evaluator dependencies and app-state specs. State classes: authoritative (DB, user files; private delta over immutable base via CoW), derived (caches, indexes; private namespace, rebuildable), ephemeral (IPC, displays; recreated on activation). Resources without a fine-grained adapter fall back to a coarse private backend; never forced into sharing.
- *State-scoped execution.* Each action carries env id + generation lease; reads see base ⊕ private delta, writes go to the private branch; callbacks and background work inherit the context. Isolation invariant: writable sets of different envs are disjoint.
- *Lifecycle.* reset/snapshot/clone/fork act on capsules. Transitions freeze the route, drain in-flight work, stage a successor capsule, then atomically publish generation g+1; stale-generation requests are rejected. N logical envs multiplexed over Q physical slots.
- Adapters: web (DB, files, queues, caches per request), visual web (+ browser profiles), desktop (fs, profile, config, display, message bus, windows).
**Verification / grading.** Unchanged official evaluators; the evaluator reads the same generation as the actions. Fidelity checked by paired inference and matched SFT+RL.
**Scale & cost.** One host (8× RTX PRO 5000, 192 cores). At 8 concurrent envs: throughput ×2.22 (WebArena), ×6.20 (VisualWebArena), ×3.08 (OSWorld); memory 2,103→464 MiB (WebArena); incremental storage 6,757→13 MiB; up to ×9,535 storage on VWA Reddit. CoW ablation on a 3.6 GB DB: create 5.48→0.07 s. Transactional publication: 0/100 mixed-generation events vs 100/100 with direct publication.
**Key results.** Success with CUA-Sandbox ≥ Docker in all 12 model–benchmark pairs (e.g. GPT-5.6-Terra OSWorld 67.29→70.62); matched SFT→RL of Qwen3.5-4B gives the same RL gain (OSWorld +3.88 vs +3.87).
**Failure modes & limits.** Depends on complete resource contracts; read-only task used for the non-DB ablation, so isolation under writes is not stressed; max density unmeasured; up to 16 concurrency only. Critique: the systematic uplift over Docker in every pair suggests the Docker baseline had flakiness (timeouts/env errors scored as failures), which is itself evidence that environment flake silently depresses measured success.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Classify every mutable resource as authoritative, derived or ephemeral and give each its own lifecycle.
- CoW deltas over an immutable base make fork/reset near-free.
- Generation-fenced leases and freeze→drain→publish avoid mixed-state observations during reset.
- Evaluator must read the same state generation as the agent's last action.
- Any resource that cannot be scoped goes private, never shared "for efficiency".
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F2/World: the authoritative/derived/ephemeral split and the resource contract are a good shape for `SeedBundle` + `StateAdapter` snapshot/restore and "one World image per domain, many tasks per snapshot" (§3.10 lever 6). G2 determinism and G10 flake rely on reset being exact; generation fencing is the mechanism. The Docker-vs-capsule gap is a reminder to log env errors separately from task failures (F12 `env_flake`). Not a task-synthesis source. Priority P2 (P1 if CUA/web tiers become a training lane).

## 2609.08404 — Environments as Scaffold: Enriching Feedback to Bootstrap Self-Evolving Agents in Long-Horizon Tasks (Yuan et al., 2026; preprint, Fudan/CASIA)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–C).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. It is a difficulty knob on the environment side (hints and richer observations) with schedule and group-consistency rules; maps to §3.6 ladders and the observability/hints knobs.
**Problem.** Long-horizon agent RL stalls on all-zero groups. Instead of SFT warm-up, make the environment's feedback richer during RL, then remove the dependence.
**Method / algorithm.** An enrichment operator o⁺_t = E(o_t, h_t) defines a Feedback-Enriched Environment (FEE) with the same goals, transitions and reward. Two kinds:
- *Action guidance (AG):* procedural hints. SciWorld: next valid actions from the expert trajectory. BFCL: a suggested action appended after the user query (also hints such as "information is missing, ask the user").
- *Observation enrichment (OE):* hidden state made visible. SciWorld: the game's internal subgoal-progress tracker printed into the observation. BFCL: expanded tool returns (current path, success status, diagnostics, unlocked-door list).
- *When:* intra-episode (early steps 1–3 vs late steps 6–10 of a 15-step episode) and inter-episode (early vs late training). Each enrichment applied with probability 0.5.
- Pilot (Qwen3-4B-Thinking, GRPO, 200 steps, 16 envs): AG > OE overall; AG works early, OE works late; schedule **AG-Early for steps 0–100, then OE-Late for 100–200** is best.
- Main runs: Qwen3-4B/8B × GRPO/DAPO/GSPO; evaluation always on the *standard* env with disjoint tasks; BFCL trains on FileSystem + VehicleControl, holds out TradingBots + TravelBooking.
- **Intra-group consistency:** all G rollouts in a group receive the same enrichment decision for a state; random per-rollout enrichment makes GRPO unstable.
**Verification / grading.** Unchanged benchmark rewards (binary). Enrichment touches observations only. Internalisation probe: multiple-choice questions on whether a tool return includes the enriched field; FEE-trained models increasingly predict the enriched output in the standard env.
**Scale & cost.** 200 RL steps per run; small benchmarks (SciWorld; BFCL v3 multi-turn 4 domains; 400 BFCL envs for the difficulty-tier analysis). No $ reported.
**Key results.** Average +2.82 pp over standard env across scales and algorithms (e.g. Qwen3-8B GSPO SciWorld 53.91→60.94; Qwen3-4B GRPO BFCL-Base 58→68). Hard-tier envs (standard-trained success <0.33) +4.3 pp. Entropy collapse delayed (standard collapses near step 250 without entropy loss, FEE stays stable for 300). **Regressions on Miss-Param (−7 pp, 4B DAPO) and Miss-Func (−4 pp, 8B GRPO):** agents trained with guidance follow cues and ask for clarification less. On AppWorld FEEs still gave zero reward.
**Failure modes & limits.** Hand-designed enrichment per env; schedule hyperparameters hand-set; gains small and per-cell noisy (single runs, peak checkpoint reported); fails when reward is extremely sparse. Critique: peak-of-curve reporting inflates gains; AG uses expert trajectories, so it is not free when no oracle exists (in Crucible the F4 oracle provides it).
**Tips & tricks.**
- Hints early in training, richer state late; withdraw hints as p̂ rises.
- Same enrichment for every rollout in a GRPO group.
- Evaluate only in the unenriched environment.
- Watch abstention/clarification skills: scaffolding erodes "question the input" behaviour.
- Expose existing internal progress trackers (milestones) as observation enrichment.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** §3.6 ladders: concrete evidence for the "hint level" and "observability" knobs and for scaffold removal on a schedule; the oracle program (F4) can supply action guidance and the milestone set (Φ) can supply observation enrichment. Rule to adopt: enrichment decision keyed by (task, step-bucket, training phase), constant within a group. Add a guard: infeasible/clarification tasks (§3.3) must be measured before and after scaffolded training because scaffolds erode them. F9: a scaffolded variant is a separate DifficultyRecord row, never counted as the base task's p̂. Priority P1.

## 2609.05837 — AgentBrew: Offline Tool-Use Agent Learning from Raw Real-World Trajectories (Lyu et al., 2026; preprint, NTU/Kuaishou)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–G).  Relevance to Part 2: medium–high for traces-to-tasks (hindsight relabelling of logs) and for an information-theoretic per-step credit that can rank which steps of a trace matter; low for verification (it has none).
**Problem.** Real apps (GitHub, Notion, Postgres via MCP) have no tasks, no verifiers, no faithful simulator, and limited interaction budget. Learn from one batch of raw, unfiltered trajectories.
**Method / algorithm.**
1. *Grounded task proposal.* LLM writes high-level categories from tool descriptions (14 GitHub, 12 Notion, 15 Postgres; multi-tool, real workflows, distinct tool combinations). Per category, an agent makes **exactly one read-only call** to find real entities and writes one natural request referencing only verified ids (writes target user-owned resources), with a style example from a different category. Schema-entity existence is validated. Substrates: 200 public repos, 180 Notion templates, 145 BIRD/Spider DBs; train and eval instances share no entities.
2. *Execution.* Qwen3-32B runs each task; trajectories hitting T_max are dropped; all others kept unfiltered (~2.5k GitHub, 2.5k Notion, 3.5k Postgres).
3. *Retrospective task inference.* Drop chain-of-thought (it was conditioned on the original goal), keep (call, observation). Extract a structured change summary of successful state-changing ops. An LLM minimally edits the original task to what was actually achieved: keep the task type, keep only completed subsets, delete rather than invent, drop strong quantifiers (all, each, exact count) unless supported.
4. *PMI credit.* With a reference LM, L_t = mean token NLL of the inferred instruction Î given the call/obs prefix up to t; L_∅ with no prefix. Credit w_t = L_{t−1} − L_t (w_0 = L_∅ − L_0); Σ w_t = PMI(Î; τ̄) exactly. Clamp negatives to 0; normalise per trajectory to [0,2] by the max; all-nonpositive → weight 0; zero-weight steps removed.
5. *Training.* Weighted SFT: −Σ w̃_t log π(a_t | Î, s_t) over the full action including CoT. Qwen3-32B is policy, reference LM and relabeller. Credit for 8.5k trajectories: ~9 h on 4×H100.
**Verification / grading.** None during training. Evaluation uses MCP-Universe/MCPMark programmatic state verifiers. Misalignment guard: when Î does not fit τ̄, credits are uniformly low (checked by GPT-5 alignment labels). Hackable in principle: PMI rewards actions that make the *description* predictable, so verbose observations that echo the instruction's words score high regardless of causal effect.
**Scale & cost.** ~8.5k trajectories; 1 epoch weighted SFT on 4×H100; 9 GPU-hours ×4 for credit.
**Key results.** Qwen3-32B average Acc 9.5→18.2 (+8.7), Score 49.0→58.7, above Qwen3-235B (15.9/54.3); rejection sampling with an LLM judge +2.8 Acc and on Notion Acc 0 because the judge rejected nearly all trajectories. Credit ablation (same relabelled data): PMI > InfoPO > uniform > LLM self-score (self-score on Notion 7.1 < uniform 10.7). Relabelling ablation on Notion: original I 7.1 → inferred Î 14.2 Acc. Adding progressively lower-quality data monotonically helps (top 15%: 10.7 → all: 14.2 Acc). Transfer to 14B: 8.7→20.2 Acc; TOUCAN-14B (1.5M synthetic trajs) and AWM-14B gave little or negative.
**Failure modes & limits.** Three apps only; long or chaotic trajectories may relabel badly; PMI is a correlational proxy. Critique: test sets are small (28/28/21 tasks judging from percentages), so ±7 pp is one task; no RL, so hindsight tasks are never re-solved or verified.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Strip CoT before relabelling a trace; it carries the original, possibly failed, intent.
- Relabel by minimal edit of the original task, deleting unsupported parts and strong quantifiers.
- Feed the relabeller a change summary of successful writes, separate from reads and failed calls.
- Ground proposals with one read-only probe and verified ids.
- PMI(instruction; prefix) differences give per-step credit without a reward model.
- LLM self-scoring of steps is worse than uniform weighting.
- An LLM judge as a trajectory filter can collapse yield to zero on complex stateful apps.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** Traces-to-tasks (§3.4): adopt the relabel rules (strip CoT, change summary from the state diff, minimal edit, drop unsupported quantifiers) for the intent labeller, but keep our rule that the task's ground truth is the rebuilt end state plus a re-solve, never the label alone. PMI credit is a cheap way to pick the "key actions" of a trace for (a) choosing which sub-goal a trace exemplifies (NNetNav-style pruning), (b) seeding milestones Φ, and (c) F12 first-wrong-step analysis as a weak prior. F3 grounding: "one read-only probe, verified ids" is a minimal grounding contract. Avoid as a verifier. Priority P1 (traces-to-tasks), P2 (PMI credit).

## 2608.27260 — What Makes Good Agentic Data? An ACE Lens on Data Generation for LLM Agents (Zeng et al., 2026; survey, Huawei/SJTU)
Read: FULL (all sections; tables skimmed).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. A survey, no experiments. Its value is vocabulary and a few crisp rules that match and sharpen Crucible's stage contracts: accuracy as a conjunctive admission gate over four factors, complexity as learner- and configuration-relative failure probability, diversity as entropy over factor slices of the *valid, in-band* subset minus behavioural redundancy.
**Problem.** Agentic data papers are organised by domain and mix construction with verification and selection, so mechanisms are hard to compare.
**Method / algorithm (framework).**
- Data object d = (E, q, τ, v): environment spec (state carrier D, tools F, rules P_rule, observation map Ω, success interface v), task signal (may be latent, revealed over turns), interaction realisation, optional verifier.
- Paradigms by anchor: forward E→q→τ; task-first; trajectory-first (Learn-by-Interact, OS-Genesis, Trajectory2Task); structure-first (tool graph or blueprint, e.g. APIGen-MT, Magnet). Self-evolving loops are cross-cutting.
- **ACE objective:** max over generator φ of λ_C·mean_{d∈B_A} g_z(C_z(d)) + λ_D·D(B_A), subject to Pr[A(d)=1] ≥ α. Utility is 0 if nothing passes accuracy.
- **Accuracy:** A(d) = V_E(E) ∧ V_q(q|E) ∧ V_τ(τ|E,q) ∧ V_v(v|E,q,τ); relational checks matter because separately generated factors drift apart.
- **Complexity:** C_z(d) = 1 − Pr[v=1 | d, z], z = model + scaffold + tools + verifier + sampling + budget. Structural features (depth, width, joins, hidden info, horizon) are explanatory controls, not difficulty. Paired criterion for agent-requiring tasks: p_{z0}(d) < ρ ≤ p_{zA}(d) (base fails, assisted config succeeds).
- **Diversity:** D(B) = Σ_k w_k H(Z_k | valid, in-band) − λ·Red(B_A), k ∈ {environment, task, interaction}. Measure: per-factor coverage Cov_k and normalised entropy H̃_k over declared categories, plus a few joint distributions to catch "template locking" (each factor diverse alone but only in fixed combinations); behavioural non-redundancy via action graphs, state transitions, Vendi-style effective number; transfer on named held-out factors; marginal learner utility.
**Verification / grading.** Survey of layered stacks: rules → execution/state → step/trajectory critics → selective human escalation. Rules stated: passing a layer is no evidence for properties that layer cannot observe; record failure class and provenance; keep alternative valid paths because repair against a fixed verifier narrows data to verifier-friendly patterns.
**Scale & cost.** N/A (survey). Cites that a smaller set of robustly verified environments can beat a larger unreliable one (EnvFactory).
**Key results.** None of its own. Synthesised trends: plausibility → execution-grounded accuracy; static heuristics → model-relative complexity; surface variation → behavioural coverage.
**Failure modes & limits.** No empirical validation of the objective; weights, entropy estimators and categories left per domain. Many citations are 2026 preprints with no reported numbers in the survey.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Validity is conjunctive across env, task, trajectory and verifier; no trade-off with difficulty or novelty.
- Report difficulty with the full configuration z (model, scaffold, tools, verifier, budget).
- Hidden information is complexity only if it has an intended source and recovery action.
- Added obligations must be checked by the verifier, otherwise they are label noise.
- Compute diversity only on valid, in-band tasks.
- Track joint distributions of factors to catch template locking.
- Treat clarification, refusal, partial completion and recovery as separate task regimes.
- Generator/model mixing is an intervention, not diversity; measure induced coverage.
- Use the paired "base fails, assisted succeeds" band to find tasks that need agentic skill.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** Cross-cutting. Adopt (a) the conjunctive admission rule as the F6 contract statement; (b) C_z with z recorded in every `DifficultyRecord` (add scaffold, tool set, budget, verifier version fields); (c) diversity meters computed post-F6 and per difficulty band, plus 2–3 declared joint slices (skill × World facet, App × answer kind) to detect template locking in §3.5's grid; (d) task regimes (clarify, refuse/infeasible, partial, recover) as an explicit axis in `DomainSpec.axes`; (e) the paired band as an F10 probe (instruction-only or no-tools config vs full config). Priority P1 (cheap, mostly schema and reporting).

## 2608.06197 — EnvACE: Internalizing Environment Dynamics via World Rehearsal for Agentic Reinforcement Learning (Xu et al., 2026; preprint, Tencent/SJTU)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–D).  Relevance to Part 2: low. A training method that removes the environment from RL; it is the opposite of Crucible's "execution decides" rule. Worth knowing as a hazard and as a test-time trick.
**Problem.** Executable environments are costly and simulators are hard to ground. EnvACE has the policy itself generate tool responses during RL.
**Method / algorithm.** One shared policy, two roles per turn: ACT emits a tool call; REHEARSE emits the environment response given history and the call, using a "precise tool simulator" prompt (format check, tool whitelist check, JSON-schema argument check with structured error codes, else reuse few-shot ground-truth results for matching calls or generate a schema-valid consistent result). The trajectory unfolds with no external environment. **Reward:** trajectory-level, from a verifiable evaluator *or a checklist LLM judge* (Qwen3-30B-A3B in training). **Role-wise GRPO:** every output in rollout i gets R_i; advantage A = R_i − mean reward over all outputs of the same role across the K rollouts; both roles update θ. Training data from CM2; Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B; 470 steps, lr 1e-6, batch 16, 4 rollouts/prompt, 30 turns max; 16×H20. Test-time: N private rehearsals (parallel or sequential with self-evaluation and suggestions) summarised into a memory, then one committed real execution.
**Verification / grading.** The training reward judges trajectories whose observations the policy wrote itself. Nothing stops the rehearsal role from producing observations that make the judge's checklist pass; the paper does not measure rehearsal fidelity against real tool returns. This is a textbook reward-hacking surface.
**Scale & cost.** 16 H20 GPUs; 470 steps. No $.
**Key results.** EnvACE-8B Overall 32.91 vs EnvScaler-8B 31.92, AWM-14B 32.54 (BFCL v4 46.04; τ² 36.7; VitaBench 16.0). Controlled vs standard GRPO at 8B on τ²: 31.2→36.7. Shared vs per-role parameters: 35.5→36.7. FinMCP TF1 46.78. Test-time parallel rehearsal N=2: overall 36.7→40.9; BFCL multi-turn 42.1 (N=1)→49.5 (N=5).
**Failure modes & limits.** ≤8B; tool-use only. Critique: gains over env-scaling baselines are ≤1 pp overall; no measurement of how often rehearsed observations are wrong; judge-based reward on self-generated worlds invites collusion between the two roles.
**Tips & tricks.**
- A schema-checking "simulator" prompt with structured error codes is a cheap stub for unbuilt tools.
- Per-role baselines when one policy plays several roles in a rollout.
- Pre-execution private rehearsal (simulate, self-evaluate, summarise, then act once) is a test-time lever.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** Avoid as a training path: violates design rule 2 (execution decides) and §3.8's anchor guards. Two narrow uses: (a) F1 debugging: a schema-validating LLM stub for tools not yet implemented, only for blind tool-test drafting, never for reward; (b) F10/F12: a policy that predicts tool returns could be probed for "imagined vs real observation" divergence as a hack detector in trace audits. Priority P2.

## 2607.05943 — SearchEyes: Towards Frontier Multimodal Deep Search Intelligence via Search World Simulation (Jiao et al., 2026; preprint, CUHK MMLab et al.)
Read: FULL (main text, App. 6–12).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. A clean instance of "one structure defines the World, the task, the verifier and the dense reward": a typed KG yields questions, a closed deterministic search World, and gold intermediate entities used as step-level credit anchors. Directly analogous to Crucible's oracle trace + milestones Φ.
**Problem.** Multimodal search-agent pipelines build data, environment and reward separately: path metadata is thrown away, training uses irreproducible live search APIs, and reward is a sparse final-answer bit.
**Method / algorithm.**
1. *World.* Intersect Wikidata5M triples, Wiki6M entity texts and Wikipedia images → ~1.2M entities (340K visual), 5.8M triples, 822 predicates mapped to 4 domains (Person/Work/Org/Geo). Remove hubs (degree > 500) and 47 meta predicates (instance_of…). Closed corpus = one summary doc per entity; text_search = BM25 + dense fused by RRF; lookup(QID); visual_search(crop) over image embeddings; summarize; sandboxed python. Deterministic; doc↔entity bijection, so the env logs exactly which entity each step retrieved.
2. *PKC path sampling.* Random walk of K∈{3,4,5} hops (P 0.3/0.5/0.2) from a visual anchor with ≥2 out-edges. Constraints: (C1) perception/knowledge hop alternation, ≥2 P-hops; (C2) ≥1 one-to-few predicate plus a disambiguating constraint edge (v_ans, r_c, v_c) that no sibling satisfies (treewidth 2); (C3) adjacent hops in different domains and ≥3 domains per path; (C4) hub exclusion, predicate blacklist, dedup by (anchor, answer) and by full entity sequence.
3. *Concealed question generation* (GPT-4o): anchor only as an image; predicates never disclosed, only one domain hint from a random middle hop; constraint entity described relationally; no path entity name or alias in the text; drop answers in the top-100 common entities or single-token answers. Five filters: degenerate path, single-hop shortcut, knowledge contamination, cross-modal grounding, answer verifiability. 22K questions (10K SFT, 12K RL).
4. *SFT trajectories with privileged conditions:* retrieval scores of gold entities ×β=3; GPT-4o-mini denoised observations; 8 samples/question, accept on exact answer (~42%); export with raw observations so the student sees the real World. 30K trajectories.
5. *HaPO.* GRPO G=8. Episode advantage from final-answer bit. **Hop anchors:** a trajectory is anchored at gold entity v_k at the first step v_k appears in its observations; within each anchor group (≥2 members, non-zero variance) compute group-relative advantage of final outcome, assign from that step onward (latest anchor wins). Final A = 0.3·A_ep + 0.7·A_hop. Fatal-aware masking: ≥3 consecutive tool errors → zero advantage after; prefix clamped to max(A,0). Sigmoid soft clipping with asymmetric temperatures. Format penalty (>10 answer tags → reward/4); repetition kill-switch.
**Verification / grading.** Training reward: normalised exact match against the KG gold answer; benchmark scoring uses GPT-4o judge. Hack surface: answer extraction fallbacks (last line) could be gamed; anchors reward *retrieving* gold entities, which a policy could exploit by broad searches that surface many entities (no penalty for over-retrieval).
**Scale & cost.** SFT 8×H20 3 epochs; RL 200 steps on 64 H20. 22K questions from one KG.
**Key results.** SearchEyes-27B avg 68.1 over six benchmarks vs OpenSearch-VL-32B 61.9; 9B 59.3. PKC ablation (avg of 4): full 61.8; −anti-shortcut 54.1 (−7.7); −P/K alternation 57.6; −retrieval boost 57.1; −concealment 59.3; −treewidth 59.3; −domain balancing 60.0; free-form GPT-4o synthesis without graph 49.2 (−12.6); Wikipedia path sampling 53.5. HaPO vs GRPO +4.0 (GiGPO +0.7, DAPO +0.3); hop-only α=0 +3.3. VisSearch Bench (1,000 held-out ≥4-hop questions): SearchEyes-27B 24.3 vs GPT-5 5.4.
**Failure modes & limits.** Training World is Wikipedia; evaluation on web benchmarks uses live Serper search (sim-to-real gap not measured). Ablations are single runs. Critique: their own benchmark is built by the same pipeline, so it measures in-distribution skill; the anchor credit counts retrieval, not use.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Keep construction metadata (the oracle path) and reuse it as milestones for dense credit.
- Closed, deterministic retrieval World with an id↔document bijection makes milestones checkable.
- Force skill alternation structurally (P/K hops) when the intent is modality switching.
- Disambiguating constraint edges (no sibling satisfies) create a verification step that cannot be skipped.
- Never name path entities; give at most one coarse domain hint.
- Privileged generation (boost gold retrieval, denoise) to get demonstrations, then export with raw observations.
- Mask advantages after consecutive tool errors; keep only positive credit on the prefix.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F4/F5: adopt "oracle trace → milestones Φ" literally: each gold intermediate entity/state the oracle touches becomes a milestone, and group-relative advantage per milestone is the in-training use (Part 1 trainer). F3/§3.6 knobs: constraint-edge (treewidth) and concealment are generic knobs ("linkage" and "obfuscation" in DomainSpec). G9 no-shortcut: the anti-shortcut ablation (−7.7) is strong evidence that shortcut filters are the highest-value gate. F10: concealment ablation is an intent probe pattern (leak entity names → measure pass-rate jump). Avoid evaluating only on a same-pipeline benchmark. Priority P1.

One turn per trajectory in the RL pool (multi-turn expansion with shared prefixes collapsed reward variance). Rejected rewards: pairwise vs initial checkpoint (slow, unstable) and "Turing test" (too many false negatives). Observed hack: **self-praise** text in predicted observations; mitigations: rule anchor, content-type classification (deterministic content judged by exact match), strict tag extraction so the thinking never reaches the judge.

Fictional DB + SQL answers give a hard ground truth for search. Leakage risk acknowledged: the simulation instruction carries the success state; they rely on OOD real-env evaluation to argue no leakage.
**Scale & cost.** 92,308 RL turns, 7,094 SFT; 10M+ CPT trajectories. No $.

In-trace next-state prediction accuracy 69.9→78.3%.

Critique: Sim RL rewards are generated rubrics; no hackability audit of the Sim RL policy; most numbers are single runs.
**Tips & tricks.**
- A learned simulator helps RL only with a detailed initial state and per-turn control instructions.
- Controlled perturbations (pagination, partial results, transient errors, batch partial failures) teach skills real environments rarely demand.
- Fictional worlds backed by a real DB and SQL answers stop parametric-memory shortcuts.
- "Snippets tease, never answer" forces deeper tool use; check the behaviour shift (extract calls up).
- Mask echo/boilerplate turns from the loss using overlap/novelty statistics; keep them as context.
- Skip retry cycles without breaking the state chain; drop no-change GUI turns.
- One prediction turn per trajectory in RL to avoid shared-prefix variance collapse.
- Judge content by type: exact for deterministic, plausibility for pre-existing, format for runtime metadata.
- Choose and tune judges by a blind real-vs-generated discrimination test.
- Keep thinking out of the judge's view; watch for self-praise in outputs.

F5 for answer tasks: fictional DB + SQL ground truth is a clean answer-check construction. F12: the four-statistic turn classifier is a cheap way to drop echo turns before failure clustering. Avoid LWM-as-reward. Note for the 35B student: LWM warm-up on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B gave +6.3 Terminal-Bench 2.0; a candidate Part 1 auxiliary objective. ## 2605.27209 — Learning to Act under Noise: Enhancing Agent Robustness via Noisy Environments (Chen et al., 2026; preprint, NUS/Meituan/USTC)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–B).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. Gives a concrete noise taxonomy (user-side and tool-side), a mixed clean/noisy GRPO group design, and a gap-triggered curriculum. Directly maps to World knobs and to G13/G10 interaction with flake.
**Problem.** Agents trained in clean synthetic environments (clear users, stable tools) degrade under real noise.
**Method / algorithm.**
- Environments from an environment-scaling pipeline (domain graph → tools + DB; GPT-4.1 builds, Claude Sonnet 4.5 verifies, GLM-4.6 writes instructions).
- *User-side noise* (applied before the episode by perturbing the simulated user's interaction pattern, keeping the task objective q): ambiguous, inconsistent (needs change), redundant, out-of-scope.
- *Tool-side noise* (applied to a random subset of tool returns during rollouts): failures (e.g., 429), incomplete/truncated, misleading/inconsistent fields, redundant detail. Injector: Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (also user simulator); reward: a trained Qwen3-32B rubric evaluator.
- *Hybrid groups:* of N rollouts per task, N_noise are noisy; advantages normalised **separately within clean and noisy subgroups**, loss over the union (GSPO base).
- *Schedule:* robustness gap Δ = success(clean) − success(noisy) on the same task; start at ρ=0; when Δ < θ=0.05, raise both noise proportion (cap ρ ≤ 0.5) and difficulty (perturbation frequency, user anomaly severity). All-pass/all-fail groups dropped.
- Training: Qwen3-8B/32B, 100 steps, 32 rollouts/sample in config (64 in main text), lr 1e-6, KL 0; 32/64 H800 for 3–5 days.
**Verification / grading.** Rubric-based learned evaluator (Qwen3-32B) in training; Claude Sonnet 4.5 as evaluator at test time, GPT-4.1 as user. No state-based checks reported. "Misleading" tool noise can make a task unsolvable or reward a wrong action; the paper asserts solvability is preserved but shows no check.
**Scale & cost.** 32–64 H800 GPUs × 3–5 days per run.
**Key results.** AgentNoiseBench-τ² Retail (Qwen3-8B) Avg@4: GSPO 31.80 → NoisyAgent 36.40; 32B 37.72 → 43.20. Clean τ² Retail 8B: 46.49 → 47.59 (small but consistent gains on clean benchmarks). Ablation (Delivery, 8B, noisy): full 21.50; noise on all rollouts 13.25 (worse than no noise 16.00); fixed difficulty 20.00. Under noise, tool calls fall 13.9→11.4 while output tokens double. In 78% of the 23 tasks the model newly solves under noise, the base model had gathered all information but never executed the final write.
**Failure modes & limits.** Training tasks are drawn from τ²-Bench and VitaBench domains that are also evaluated (filtered by pass rate), so train/test separation is unclear. Judge-based reward throughout. Noise types not exhaustive; no compositional noise.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Mix clean and perturbed rollouts in one group; normalise advantages per subgroup.
- Never perturb every rollout; full-noise training was worse than no noise.
- Raise noise when the clean–noisy success gap closes (Δ < 0.05).
- User noise taxonomy: ambiguous, inconsistent, redundant, out-of-scope.
- Tool noise taxonomy: failure, truncation, misleading field, redundant output.
- Watch the "gathered everything but never wrote" failure; it is what noise exposes.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** §3.6 knobs / DomainSpec: add `noise` knobs with these eight classes, implemented as World-side perturbation operators on Apps and `ActorSpec` (not LLM-generated returns), each tagged *solvability-preserving* or not; misleading-field noise must be paired with a second source of truth in the World so the task stays communicable (G8). F6: G2 determinism and G10 flake must run with noise seeds fixed, so injected failures are reproducible and are not counted as flake. F9: report per-student "noise gap" Δ as part of `DifficultyRecord`; a task in band clean but far below band noisy is a robustness task, not a capability task. Part 1 trainer: subgroup-normalised advantages when a group mixes knob settings. Priority P1.

## 2604.05336 — TRACE: Capability-Targeted Agentic Training (Kang et al., 2026; preprint, Stanford)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–K; generated env code skimmed).  Relevance to Part 2: high. This is "trace-based failure analysis → targeted environment synthesis → per-skill RL" end to end, with a quantitative contrastive rule for picking skills and an environment-generation contract that states the difficulty band and variance targets. Closest published analogue of §3.4 point 6 (failure clusters → F3 variants) and of F12.
**Problem.** Agents fail because of a few missing capabilities; RL on the target environment learns them sample-inefficiently, and untargeted synthetic data wastes compute on known skills.
**Method / algorithm.**
1. *Rollouts* of the base agent in the target environment (τ²-Bench airline+retail; for SWE, the disjoint SWE-smith split; ToolSandbox).
2. *Contrastive capability identification.* (a) Discovery: an LLM analysis agent reads tool calls, results and final replies and writes a canonical dictionary of named capabilities. (b) Labelling with the fixed dictionary: for each trajectory and capability, label NA / PRESENT / LACKING. Error rates ER⁺(c) on successes and ER⁻(c) on failures (excluding NA). Contrastive gap Δ(c) = ER⁻ − ER⁺; failure coverage Cov(c) = share of failed trajectories where c is LACKING. Keep c if **Δ ≥ 0.20 and Cov ≥ 0.10**, and only if selected consistently across repeated labelling runs (10 runs; top 3 in all 10, tool-calling precision in 8/10). ~5 min per analysis pass.
3. *Environment synthesis* per capability by a coding agent given the capability, failure patterns and failed trajectories. Contract (App. E prompt): implement a GameEnv protocol; same tool schemas as the target; **reset(seed) deterministic, different seeds meaningfully different**, randomised DB contents, requests, constraint combinations, distractor counts; one skill per env (≤2); ground-truth solution per seed; reward from final DB state (hash check) and correct communicated result; **target base success 30–60%** (>80% too easy, <15% too hard); multi-level rewards (0, .2, .5, .8, 1) with reward = α·Π components + (1−α)·Σ w·components; **self-check with 100+ rollouts on a vLLM server: mean 0.3–0.6, std > 0.2, >60% of groups with variance.**
4. *RL:* one LoRA per capability (GRPO, K rollouts per seed, groups with zero variance dropped; lr 1e-5, ≤40 iterations; 4–8 A100). 
5. *Composition:* token-level MoE gate over frozen adapters (only gates trained; CE + load-balance loss λ=0.05; top-1 at inference).
**Verification / grading.** Synthetic envs: programmatic final-state hash + answer check against the seeded ground truth. Capability labels: LLM judgments (no human audit reported). Hack surface: partial-credit components could be farmed; the env author sees failed trajectories, so the env may overfit to benchmark surface (prompt warns against it).
**Scale & cost.** 2–4 capability envs per benchmark; 5,120 rollouts to reach 48.2 on τ²; adapters 1.6B params each (5.3%).
**Key results.** τ² overall (Qwen3-30B-A3B): base 32.9; GRPO on target 37.8; AWM 38.4; GEPA (prompting the same capabilities) 39.6; single-capability adapter 40.3; TRACE 48.2. Qwen3.6-27B: 50.0→59.1. SWE-bench Verified (30B): 26.0→41.0 (SWE-RL 32.6); Qwen3.6-27B 68.0→73.2. Under equal rollouts TRACE beats GRPO by +10.4; GRPO on target unstable (dropped to 35.4 at 3,840 rollouts). Consolidation: MoE 48.2 ≫ multi-capability GRPO 40.9, SFT 37.8, on-policy distillation 37.8, TIES-style merge 39.6. ToolSandbox 0.411→0.560 mean similarity; τ²-trained TRACE transfers to ToolSandbox better than τ²-trained GRPO.
**Failure modes & limits.** On τ², discovery rollouts come from the same benchmark tasks that are evaluated (only SWE uses a disjoint split), so capability selection sees test failures. Single greedy run in main tables (App. G adds 3 seeds at T=0.7). Capability labels are LLM-made. Critique: an env "where success depends primarily on c" is asserted, not tested by ablation.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Fix a capability dictionary first, then label; repeat labelling and keep only stable selections.
- Rank skills by contrastive gap (failures vs successes) and failure coverage, not by failure count.
- Thresholds that worked: gap ≥ 0.20, coverage ≥ 0.10.
- Generated env contract: seeded determinism, one skill, same tool schemas, ground truth per seed.
- Builder must self-verify the reward distribution with ≥100 rollouts before handing off.
- Targets: base mean reward 0.3–0.6, std > 0.2, >60% informative groups.
- Multiplicative-plus-additive-floor reward for co-required behaviours.
- Per-skill adapters + learned router beat training one adapter on the mixture.
- Prompting the identified skills (GEPA) helps but much less than training them.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F12 → F3 (traces-to-tasks via failure clusters): adopt the contrastive skill selector over `failure_skill` labels: compute Δ(c) and Cov(c) over student rollouts per family, keep skills with Δ ≥ 0.2, Cov ≥ 0.1, stable over ≥3 relabel runs by a different-family labeller; emit `FeedbackSignal.failure_clusters` with these numbers. Then F3 proposals "variant of family F targeting skill c" with the TRACE env contract mapped to our gates: seeded determinism (G2), ground truth per seed (F4 oracle), single-skill intent (F10 single-subtask probe), builder-side pre-check of the reward distribution (a cheap pre-F9 sanity run on the frontier, not the student). F10: the GEPA-vs-training result is a useful "intent needs training" control. Avoid: discovery on benchmark tasks (our sealed split must never feed the selector). Part 1: per-skill LoRA + router is a training-side option, P2. Priority P0 for the contrastive selector, P1 for the env contract.

## 2509.17158 — ARE: scaling up agent environments and evaluations (Meta Superintelligence Labs, 2025; preprint; introduces Gaia2)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–B; per-model tables partly lost in HTML).  Relevance to Part 2: high. ARE is the closest existing design to Crucible's World (apps, events, clock, notifications, people) and its verifier (oracle write-action DAG with hard/soft argument checks, causality and timing) is directly reusable for F5 path and timing checks. It also documents a real RL hack of an LLM soft check and a "verify the verifier" protocol.

- *Abstractions:* Apps (stateful tool collections; Python methods auto-exposed as tools; decorators mark `read` vs `write`; tools role-scoped to agent/user/env); Environment (apps + rules; deterministic given state and seed); Events (agent/user/env tool calls, *conditional* events that poll a predicate, *validation* events that fail the run on timeout, *oracle* events = ground-truth actions), scheduled in a DAG with absolute or relative timing, all logged; Notifications (policy whitelist; low/medium/high verbosity = observability knob); Scenarios = initial state + scheduled events + verification. System app: get_current_time, wait, wait_for_next_notification (simulation jumps event-to-event when the agent waits). Optional natural-language *hints* (step-by-step solution) for QA and for RL when tasks are too hard.

Known gaps: temporal consistency across apps, relationship consistency, cross-modal references.

Guardrails in the editor (turns end with send_message_to_user, connected DAG, etc.). QA: annotator A writes prompt+oracle; B and C independently solve blind; D checks consistency and rejects or tightens the prompt. **Scale & cost.** 10 universes × ~800K tokens; 1,120 scenarios; 4-annotator QA per scenario. Cost per scenario reported per model (figure).

Search hit an annotation ceiling: annotators could not outpace models.
**Failure modes & limits.** Unique-write assumption penalises valid alternatives; human-annotated oracles do not scale; read-only reasoning unverified except final answer; universe coherence partial. Authors recommend simple tasks in complex environments rather than complex tasks in simple ones.
**Tips & tricks.**

- Verify by mapping oracle write DAG to agent writes with hard (ids) and soft (free text) checks plus causality and timing.
- Release the next turn only if the current turn verifies.
- Validate the verifier on ~450 human-labelled trajectories and on perturbed-oracle unit tests.

- Generate universes in app-dependency order from a persona root; dependent apps query existing state.

- Augment existing scenarios with invariance-preserving transforms (noise, app-agents) for new capability splits.

## 2501.10893 — Learn-by-interact: A Data-Centric Framework for Self-Adaptive Agents in Realistic Environments (Su et al., 2025; ICLR 2025, Google/HKU)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–E; tables partly lost in HTML).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. The canonical "backward construction" (hindsight instruction per sub-trajectory) recipe for traces-to-tasks, with a committee filter. It produces SFT/ICL data, not verified RL tasks.
**Problem.** Agents lack environment-specific data; forward-generated instructions often do not match what the LLM's trajectory actually did.
**Method / algorithm (Alg. 1).**
1. For each document from environment resources (docs, tutorials, FAQs; or same-domain substitutes, e.g., Amazon help pages for WebArena's shop), self-instruct N=3 task instructions (generator Claude-3.5-Sonnet).
2. Reset env; roll the LLM out on each instruction to the end → trajectory T = (o0, a1, o1, …).
3. *Backward construction:* for **every contiguous sub-trajectory** T[i:j] (quadratic in length), the LLM writes a new instruction, either a summary of the steps or an abstraction of the purpose; the full-trajectory abstraction replaces the original goal when the run went astray.
4. *Filters:* drop consecutive duplicate (a, o) pairs (invalid actions/inactivity); **LLM committee** (Gemini-1.5-Pro + Claude-3.5-Sonnet) keeps a pair only if all judge it coherent, natural, reasonable and aligned.
5. *Use:* SFT (LoRA on Codegemma-7B, Codestral-22B) or ICL with *agentic retrieval*: observation-based (BM25 match of the current observation against stored trajectories, m1=5) + model-based (LLM writes a query from instruction, history, observation; dense retrieval, m2=5).

**Scale & cost.** Documents → raw trajectories → pairs → filtered: SWE-bench 6,464 → 4,568 → 41,237 → 10,232; WebArena 3,578 → 3,967 → 32,319 → 10,456; OSWorld 7,362 → 1,125 → 19,688 → 11,782; Spider2-V 11,231 → 1,226 → 21,525 → 10,169 (filter keeps 25–60%). Many LLM calls; no $.
**Key results.** ICL gains up to +12.2 (Claude-3.5); OSWorld Claude 12.4 → 22.5. Training Codestral-22B WebArena 4.7 → 24.2 (+19.5). Backward construction worth up to +14.0 in training over plain distillation (forward pairs gave ≤2 pts in ICL). Short sub-trajectories (<5 steps) help most; mixing lengths best. Agentic retrieval > instruction-only RAG; data scaling helps training more than ICL.
**Failure modes & limits.** Needs documentation; heavy LLM cost; no correctness check of relabelled pairs. Critique: for SWE-bench the "documents" are the non-repeated Python files of SWE-bench Verified itself, so synthesis reads test-repository code (contamination risk); committee is same-generator family (Claude judges Claude).
**Tips & tricks.**
- Relabel every contiguous sub-trajectory, not only the whole run.
- When a run diverges, abstract its actual purpose as the new task.
- Drop repeated identical (action, observation) steps before relabelling.
- Short sub-trajectories are the most reusable training units.
- Retrieve demonstrations by current observation, not only by instruction.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** Traces-to-tasks (§3.4): sub-trajectory relabelling multiplies candidate tasks per trace; each candidate must then be (a) rebuilt as start state = World snapshot at step i, (b) goal = state diff at step j, (c) re-solved and gated (F4–F6), which turns their SFT pairs into verifiable tasks. Short (<5-step) sub-goals map to "single-subtask" items useful for F10's single-subtask probe and for ladders (§3.6). Avoid committee-only acceptance and avoid deriving tasks from benchmark repositories (sealed split rule). Priority P1.

## 2503.07826 — Magnet: Multi-turn Tool-use Data Synthesis and Distillation via Graph Translation (Yin et al., 2025; preprint, Google/UCLA)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A prompts; App. C contamination study referenced, not detailed in HTML).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. A structure-first generator: function-dependency graph → random-walk signature path → node operations that inject specific multi-turn challenges → back-and-forth translation to queries plus executable reference calls. The node operations are reusable knobs; the hint-based positive/negative trajectories are an SFT/DPO trick.
**Problem.** Multi-turn function calling fails on nested calls, long dependencies across turns, and missing functions/parameters; teachers are too weak on multi-turn to distil directly.
**Method / algorithm.**
1. *Function pool:* 5,011 executable APIs from StableToolBench (only parameterised, execution-verified) and BFCL-v3 multi-turn implementations (names and descriptions rewritten; implementation kept). LLM labels 49 categories and tool classes.
2. *Local dependency graph:* for each function, sample 30 candidates from the same category/class; an LLM marks a directed edge when the source's output is a precondition for, equal to, or part of the target's input (cross-domain allowed).
3. *Initial signature path:* random walk of S=7 steps on out-edges.
4. *Node operations:* **Merge** consecutive turns into one multi-call turn (p=0.3; short dependency); **Insert** a nested function the query does not name (checked by an LLM "can these nest?" prompt), or insert it later for long dependency; **Split** to create an empty turn labelled miss-func or miss-param, where the correct behaviour is to ask. Apply Merge→Insert, then Split to make a second variant.
5. *Back-and-forth translation (per turn):* back-translate signatures to a user query that refers to prior outputs indirectly ("the location you just found") and never names functions; forth-translate the query + signature + last outputs to an executable call; execute to get outputs for the next turn.
6. *Trajectories:* teacher (Gemini-1.5-Pro-002) answers with a hidden [Hint] of the reference calls (context distillation) → positives; negatives: sample the SFT model 10×, an LLM judge labels turns with one of 5 error types (nested, short dep, long dep, wrong summary, missed func/param), then those wrong calls are injected as misleading hints. Post-process: shuffle tool order; drop trajectories with failure keywords ("Bad request", "does not match"); mix single-turn, parallel, multiple and irrelevance data.
7. *Training:* SFT on 34K, then multi-turn DPO + SFT loss on 4,556 pairs (Qwen2.5-Coder 7B/14B).
**Verification / grading.** Reference calls are executed; trajectory acceptance is keyword filter + judge; evaluation on BFCL-v3 and ToolQuery (60 cases, functions unseen).
**Scale & cost.** 38,556 training items; no $.
**Key results.** Magnet-14B-mDPO BFCL-v3 overall 68.01 (rank 4 at the time), multi-turn +30 over base Qwen2.5-Coder-14B, above the teacher Gemini-1.5-Pro (multi-turn 20.75); ToolQuery 73.30. Ablation: dependency graph alone ~+8 multi-turn; Merge and Insert add most on base multi-turn; Split helps miss-func/miss-param; GPT-4o teacher ≈ Gemini; self-teaching works with a small drop. Irrelevance-data ratio sweep (6.7–26.3%) trades multi-turn vs irrelevance accuracy.

**Tips & tricks.**
- Build a function dependency graph from I/O compatibility and random-walk it for coherent multi-step tasks.
- Inject named challenges as graph operations: merge (multi-call turn), insert (implicit nested call, long dependency), split (missing function/parameter → must ask).
- Back-translated queries should reference earlier outputs indirectly and never name tools.
- Hidden reference hints lift a weak teacher's multi-turn success; misleading hints from real student errors make sharp negatives.
- Shuffle tool order in prompts.
- Tune the share of irrelevance/missing-info examples; too many hurts execution.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F1/F3: the tool-dependency graph (`AppBundle.tool_graph`, already in our schema) plus Magnet's operations give generic knobs: `linkage` (insert for long dependency), `hidden_steps` (implicit nested call), `missing_info` (split → infeasible/clarify task type). F5: their five error types are a ready negative-operator list for F5 (missing nested call ≈ drop a step; wrong dependency ≈ wrong value; missed clarification ≈ infeasible-task negative). ## 2506.11045 — Procedural Environment Generation for Tool-Use Agents (Sullivan et al., 2025; EMNLP 2025, Saarland; "RandomWorld")
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–D).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. A fully procedural, type-driven generator of callable tools and non-linear tasks with exact-match goal states; solution-first by construction. Weak on realism (tool outputs are random typed values), strong on construction rules (dead-step trimming, instruction informativeness check, leakage control).
**Problem.** Tool-use datasets are rarely interactive and compositional at once, so they cannot feed online RL.
**Method / algorithm.**
1. *Type system:* 73 fine-grained base types (month-name, movie-title, price…) in a subtype hierarchy (actor-name ≤ person-name ≤ string), each with a description, a **generator** (samples values) and a **recognizer** (type-checks inputs); constructors list(t), dict(t,u) (contravariant key), union(t,u); generators/recognizers inherited.
2. *Tools:* sample input and output types; GPT-4o writes a name and description; GPT-4o rates plausibility 1–5, keep ≥ 4 (~11% kept). A tool returns generator samples of its output types, **memoised per input** so it is deterministic within an environment; invalid input types return an error. Hand-made tools allowed (6 calculator tools, dependently typed add etc.). Optional app login with password-manager tool (credentials given in the instruction with probability p_g).
3. *Trajectory skeleton (solution first):* sample user-input types; sample length ℓ ∈ [ℓ_min, ℓ_max]; repeatedly add a tool whose inputs are all subtypes of variables already available; last tool's output = goal. **Trim** every call whose outputs do not feed (directly or indirectly) the goal and resample until all ℓ calls are used. Deduplicate skeletons.
4. *Environment:* sample user inputs, compute each call's outputs (stored).
5. *Instruction:* GPT-4o writes it from tool descriptions, user input values and the skeleton with all intermediate values replaced by variables (no output leakage). **Informativeness check:** a GPT-4o solver with privileged help (type signatures and examples, only the needed tools, in gold order, no login) must reach the goal; otherwise discard (~60% environment yield after dedup).
6. *Agent interface:* tool inventory = needed tools + distractors at ratio r_dist=1.0; TRL text environments; GRPO with **exact match of the returned value to the goal state**; SFT from the skeleton.
**Verification / grading.** Exact match on the final value. Hack risk is low for the reward itself, but tool names/descriptions can leak the chain (App. C analysis); goal values are random strings, so no semantic shortcut, but the agent can also guess formats.
**Scale & cost.** 12,000 environments from 556 tools (550 generated + 6 hand-made), ℓ 2–8; total OpenAI cost ~$150. Test: 276 envs from 75 unseen tools.
**Key results.** Qwen-RW-SFT sets NESTFUL SoTA on F1-function 0.96 and F1-param 0.71; Llama-RW-GRPO doubles base on RandomWorld test (0.483→0.721) and NESTFUL F1F (0.53→0.89). Mixed transfer: Qwen-RW-GRPO hurts ToolQA-Easy; over-fitting on the in-distribution test (Llama-SFT 0.855) correlates with worse transfer → suggests early stopping near 0.72 in-distribution accuracy and raising ℓ instead. Scaling: number of tasks matters more than number of tools in-distribution; fewer tools helped ToolQA-Hard (+15%).
**Failure modes & limits.** Random outputs make tasks semantically empty; instruction verifier may filter the hardest tasks (its failure is not task infeasibility); 7–8B only; leakage via tool names.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Build the oracle first as a typed call DAG, then write the instruction from it.
- Trim calls that do not contribute to the goal; resample to keep length fixed.
- Replace intermediate values by variables when the instruction writer sees the solution.
- Verify each instruction with a privileged solver (only needed tools, gold order); drop if it fails.
- Memoise tool outputs per input to keep environments deterministic.
- Distractor tools at a fixed ratio; length range as the curriculum knob.
- Stop training when in-distribution accuracy saturates; raise structural difficulty instead.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F4: "construction before sampling" exactly; adopt dead-step trimming as an F4 oracle lint (every oracle step must contribute to the goal end state or an answer) and as an F8 normaliser before computing behaviour signatures. F3/F5: the privileged informativeness check is G8 communicability made executable: a frontier solver given only the needed tools in order must reach the goal from the instruction, otherwise the instruction is under-specified (cheap, pre-F9). Instruction writer sees variables, not values (G11 leak rule). §3.6 knobs: ℓ range and distractor ratio. Avoid random-valued tools for our World; our Apps hold coherent state. Priority P1.

## 2510.24684 — SPICE: Self-Play In Corpus Environments Improves Reasoning (Liu et al., 2025; preprint, FAIR at Meta/NUS)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–E).  Relevance to Part 2: medium–high for the self-play source (§3.4) and difficulty (§3.6): it isolates *grounding* and *information asymmetry* as what sustains self-play, and compares four challenger rewards head-to-head. Domain is QA, not agents.
**Problem.** Ungrounded self-play (proposer and solver share one knowledge base) plateaus or collapses from hallucination amplification and information symmetry.
**Method / algorithm (Alg. 1).**
- One model, two roles. *Challenger* sees a sampled document (20K docs: 50% Nemotron-CC-Math, 50% NaturalReasoning/DCLM; segments ≤5,992 tokens) and proposes (q, a*) with a* extracted from the document; a format-selection prompt picks MCQ (4 options, balanced distractors) or free-form with typed answer (integer, expression, string); the prompt walks through complex extraction, difficulty enhancement and a self-test that the question is solvable *without* the document. Up to N=1024 attempts per document; one valid task kept per document.
- *Reasoner* answers q without the document (boxed answer); reward 1[â = a*] via Math-Verify.
- *Challenger reward:* with G=8 Reasoner samples and l_i the correctness bits, r_C = exp(−(Var(l) − 0.25)² / (2·0.01)) for valid q (peaks at p = 0.5), penalty ρ = −0.1 for invalid/unparseable questions.
- *Optimisation:* DrGRPO with role-specific advantages (mean-centred, no std normalisation), shared weights, no KL; T=640 iterations, batch 128, 8×H200.
**Verification / grading.** Rule-based answer equivalence against the document-extracted answer. Validity of a* depends on the extraction being correct; MCQs reduce verification noise. Hack surface: the Challenger could pick trivially extractable facts phrased obscurely (variance reward caps this), or produce questions whose a* is wrong but consistently "wrong" for the Reasoner too (not measured).
**Scale & cost.** 640 × 128 tasks; 8 H200 per run.
**Key results.** Qwen3-4B-Base 35.8 → 44.9 (+9.1) overall; Qwen3-8B 43.0 → 48.7; OctoThinker-3B 14.7 → 25.2; OctoThinker-8B 20.5 → 32.4. Beats R-Zero (+3.7, which degrades after 5 iterations), Absolute Zero (+4.9), and a fixed strong Challenger Qwen3-32B (+7.2). Without corpus: 40.7 vs 43.9. Fixed Challenger trains less. Co-evolution: against a fixed Reasoner, later Challengers' pass rate falls 55% → 35%; against a fixed Challenger, later Reasoners rise 55% → 85%. Challenger reward ablation: variance (Gaussian at p=0.5) > R-Zero's 1−2|p−0.5| > threshold (0<k<8) > Absolute Zero's 1−p. Mixed MCQ + free-form > either alone; each corpus helps its own domain, both together best.
**Failure modes & limits.** QA only; answer extraction errors unmeasured; single runs. Critique: the "information asymmetry" is document access only; the agentic analogue needs privileged World state.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Ground every proposal in external material the solver cannot see.
- Reward the proposer for p near 0.5 with a smooth (Gaussian-in-variance) reward; reward 1−p is the worst option.
- Penalise invalid proposals with a small negative constant.
- Per-role mean-centred advantages; no std normalisation.
- Train the proposer too; a fixed strong proposer is weaker than a co-trained one.
- Mix answer formats; MCQ stabilises verification, free-form trains reasoning.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** Self-play source (§3.4) and §3.8 task loop: the agentic analogue is "proposer sees the World snapshot and privileged notes; solver sees only the instruction and tools" (our information-separation rule 5). The variance reward ranking is evidence for the band choice in §3.6: centre ~0.5 for G=8 when the target is learning signal (vs SSR's 0.2). Use the Gaussian-variance score as the F9 *ranking* utility for in-band tasks (`archive_score` in F11). Grounding = sampled World slice + persona + cell (already §3.5 item 3). Priority P1 (in-training self-play P2).

## 2511.16043 — Agent0: Unleashing Self-Evolving Agents from Zero Data via Tool-Integrated Reasoning (Xia et al., 2025; preprint v2 2026, UNC/Stanford)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–C).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. Proposer–solver co-evolution with a tool-use reward and a diversity penalty. The key lesson for Crucible is negative: rewards come from majority-vote pseudo-labels (no ground truth), which the paper patches with ambiguity scaling; its proposer reward terms (uncertainty, tool use, repetition penalty) are reusable.
**Problem.** Self-evolution without data plateaus because generated tasks stay within the model's own ability and are single-round.
**Method / algorithm.** Two agents from the same base (Qwen3-4B/8B-Base), alternating per iteration (3 iterations):
- *Curriculum agent* (GRPO) generates one competition-style math question (prompt: "fewer than 30% of strong students could solve"). Reward R_C = format · max(0, λ_unc·R_unc + λ_tool·R_tool − R_rep), with R_unc = 1 − 2|p̂ − 0.5| where p̂ = executor self-consistency (share of k=10 answers equal to the majority); R_tool = γ·min(#tool calls, C=4) (λ_tool = 0.6); R_rep = λ_rep·|cluster|/B, clusters by 1 − BLEU distance below τ.
- *Executor* (multi-turn with a sandboxed Python interpreter, stop-and-go on code blocks) trains only on frozen-curriculum tasks with |p̂ − 0.5| ≤ 0.25 (p̂ ∈ [0.3, 0.8]); reward = 1[answer = majority-vote pseudo-label]. **ADPO:** advantage scaled by an increasing function of p̂ (trust confident labels more); upper clip bound ε_high(x) decreasing in p̂ (looser trust region on ambiguous tasks).
**Verification / grading.** None external during training; pseudo-labels by majority vote of the executor itself. Evaluation uses benchmark answers with a GPT-4o equivalence judge. Hack surface: the executor and curriculum can converge on consistently wrong answers; tool-call reward can be farmed (capped at 4).
**Scale & cost.** VeRL; k=10 samples per task; 3 iterations; hardware not given in the HTML.
**Key results.** Qwen3-8B-Base math avg 49.2 → 58.2 (R-Zero 54.7, Absolute Zero 52.6, Socratic-Zero 56.1 which uses OpenAI APIs); 4B 42.6 → 52.5. General reasoning (SuperGPQA, MMLU-Pro, BBEH) also up. Ablations: untrained curriculum −9.3; no tool reward −7.2; no repetition penalty large drop (esp. general tasks); plain GRPO instead of ADPO −1.9; multi-turn matters for hard math. Across iterations the iteration-1 executor's pass rate on newer curricula falls and tool calls per task rise.
**Failure modes & limits.** Math only; pseudo-label noise; base models only. Critique: "peak performance during training" is what is bolded in tables (checkpoint selection on test).
**Tips & tricks.**
- Proposer reward = uncertainty peak at 0.5 + capped reward for required tool use − batch repetition penalty.
- Penalise proposals by the size of their BLEU cluster in the batch.
- Down-weight advantages on low-consistency (likely mislabelled) items.
- Loosen the upper clip bound for ambiguous items to let rare correct paths grow.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** §3.4 self-play (P2) and F3 proposer reward: if Crucible ever trains a proposer, adopt the composite reward but replace pseudo-labels with F4 executed oracles and F5 checks (Agent0's weakness is exactly what construction-before-sampling fixes). The repetition penalty is a cheap in-batch F8 analogue; "reward required tool use" maps to rewarding proposals whose oracle touches the intended App set (behaviour signature match, F7). Priority P2.

## 2410.15226 — On the Diversity of Synthetic Data and its Impact on Training Large Language Models (Chen et al., 2024; preprint, CMU/Microsoft Research)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–F skimmed for prompts and settings).  Relevance to Part 2: low–medium. Pretraining-scale study (350M/1.4B) with an LLM-based clustering diversity metric. Useful as a design for an LLM-judged *cluster count* meter and for evidence that seeds × few generations per seed beats many generations per seed; not about agent tasks.
**Problem.** Text diversity metrics (n-gram, compression, perplexity, k-means on embeddings) fail to distinguish synthetic corpora that differ in their underlying topic distribution.
**Method / algorithm (LLM Cluster-agent).**
1. *Criteria discovery:* sample J=5 items, ask an LLM for 3–5 metadata attributes and scoring metrics; repeat M=100 times; a gathering prompt keeps the most frequent; a summary prompt writes high-level clustering criteria.
2. *Clustering:* sample K=10 items, the LLM clusters them by those criteria with reasons; a verification prompt removes invalid clusters; repeat N=5,000 times.
3. *Score:* D = (1/N) Σ C_i / S_i with C_i clusters and S_i samples per cluster in round i (higher = more diverse). Bootstrapped over 10 rounds of 1M-sample subsets.
- *Generation study:* 620K Wikipedia-derived topics (hierarchy + keywords via GPT-4); GPT-4o textbook-style generation with prompt variants Topic, Topic+Styles, Topic+Styles+Persona, Multi-Topic+Styles+Persona; models GPT-4o, GPT-3.5, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B; real:synthetic ratios; pretrain on 34B real + synthetic tokens, then SFT (Alpaca-GPT4 + FLANv2); 8 commonsense benchmarks.
**Verification / grading.** N/A (diversity measurement); the LLM cluster verification step is a self-check.
**Scale & cost.** Up to ~29B synthetic tokens per variant; 350M trained on 50B tokens, 1.4B on 150B.
**Key results.** LLM cluster score correlates linearly with both pretraining and SFT accuracy; baselines give inconsistent orderings (k-means even ranks 300K topics below 100K). More topics → more diverse; more generations per topic first helps then saturates/hurts. Personas + styles raise diversity and accuracy; multi-topic prompts reduce diversity (redundancy); Cosmopedia v0.2's "optimised" prompts are less diverse and worse than v0.1. Stronger generators produce more diverse data; mixing generators helps slightly. Real:synthetic near balance best; heavy synthetic over-weighting hurts. Larger models need more diversity; diversity in pretraining helps SFT even when pretraining metrics saturate.
**Failure modes & limits.** Small models; commonsense benchmarks only; the metric needs ~5,000 LLM calls per measurement and is LLM-dependent (ablation says robust across GPT-4/4o/3.5/Llama-3.1).
**Tips & tricks.**
- Let an LLM derive clustering criteria from samples before clustering; add a verification pass.
- Measure diversity on many small random batches and average.
- Prefer many seeds with few generations each over many generations per seed.
- Personas and styles diversify; asking the model to combine several seeds can reduce diversity.
- Prompt "optimisation" can silently lower diversity; measure it.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** §3.5 meters: an LLM cluster-count meter is a candidate fifth meter for *intent/scenario* diversity where embeddings fail, computed per batch on instructions and on trace summaries (cluster count per 10-item draw). Cheap evidence for "many families × few instances" (§3.10) and for quota per seed (cap generations per cell/seed). Do not use as a gate; use for monitoring generator collapse alongside family entropy. Priority P2.

## 2310.13032 — Quality-Diversity through AI Feedback (Bradley, Dai et al., 2023; ICLR 2024; "QDAIF")
Read: FULL (main text; App. A.1–A.10, A.20, A.31 read; remaining appendix tables skimmed).  Relevance to Part 2: medium. The template for an LLM-judged MAP-Elites archive: LLM as mutation operator, LLM as quality judge, LLM as diversity-axis classifier. Shows where LLM-judged quality is hackable (top of the score range) and that axes can be added mid-search.
**Problem.** Quality-diversity search needs hand-coded quality and diversity measures, impossible for subjective domains (creative writing).
**Method / algorithm.** MAP-Elites with LM operators (OpenELM):
1. *Init:* few-shot prompting from 3 hand-chosen seeds spanning the diversity axis (or zero-shot).
2. *Loop (2,000 iterations):* pick a random elite; mutate by LMX-Near (concatenate a few archive members after "Here is a random example of…", 13B model) or, for poetry with GPT-4, *LMX-rewrite* ("inspired by this poem, write a new one with a different genre and tone") or *LMX-guided* (target bin named).
3. *Evaluate:* quality = log-prob ratio of "yes"/"no" to "is this a high-quality text on topic?"; diversity = log-prob of opposing labels (positive/negative sentiment; romance/horror; happy/tragic) → position on an axis; or categorical labels from GPT-4 (5 genres × 5 tones).
4. *Discretise:* 20 bins per axis, **non-uniform, denser at the ends** (LM logit calibration is non-linear).
5. *Insert* if the bin is empty or the new item beats its elite. Metric: QD score = Σ best quality per bin.
- *Axis expansion (A.10):* start 1D, add the second axis at iteration 1,000 of 2,000 → approaches full-2D QD score with better best-solution quality than starting in 2D.
**Verification / grading.** LLM judge only, validated by humans: 280 annotations; human–AI label agreement 73% overall, 82% where humans agree, 95% on non-neutral labels; human inter-annotator 73%. **Reward hacking signal:** human quality correlates with AI fitness except in the top range (fitness 0.995–1.0) where it decouples. Embedding-based feedback (QDEF) was more hackable than LLM feedback.
**Scale & cost.** 2,000 iterations × 5 seeds per domain (poetry 200 iterations with GPT-4).
**Key results.** QDAIF beats Fixed-Few-Shot, Shuffling-Few-Shot, Random-Search and quality-only LMX on QD score in all domains; quality-only search is no better than fixed prompts. Human QD score 0.772 (vs 0.767 fixed few-shot, 0.650 quality-only). Poetry QD 130 vs random 76 vs fixed-seed-rewrite 99; LMX-rewrite misses limericks unless guided. Code (HumanEval 88): QD 47 vs 43, 53% of QDAIF solutions avoid bubble sort vs 5% for direct sampling; no method found "highly optimised" code (model bias).
**Failure modes & limits.** Axes must be specified; mis-specified axes produce pathologies (hero-spy vs hero-politician stories drop the interaction). LLM-judge hacking at extreme scores. Creative domains only (plus a small code test).
**Tips & tricks.**
- Use the archive itself as few-shot context for mutation (LMX); rewrite-to-different-bin works for strong models.
- Name the target bin when an unguided operator leaves bins empty.
- Use non-uniform bins when the axis comes from model logits.
- Distrust the top of an LLM quality scale; spot-check elites with humans.
- Add diversity axes mid-search rather than starting with the full grid.
- Quality-only selection does not produce diversity; you need per-bin elites.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F11 archive: QDAIF is the reference for "cell elite by quality" with LLM-labelled axes; in Crucible the axes are *measured* from behaviour signatures (F7), so LLM labels are only for axes without a structural measure (e.g. persona tone, request phrasing). Adopt: LMX-style proposals seeded with current cell elites (F3 "K nearest tasks"), guided proposals that name an empty cell, staged axis expansion when a DomainSpec gains a new axis, and a human spot check of top-scoring elites (part of the 10% κ queue). Avoid using an LLM quality score as the elite criterion; use learnability × interestingness (OMNI-EPIC card). Priority P1.

## 2003.08536 — Enhanced POET: Open-Ended Reinforcement Learning through Unbounded Invention of Learning Challenges and their Solutions (Wang et al., 2020; ICML 2020, Uber AI)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A).  Relevance to Part 2: medium (conceptual, high leverage). Two ideas transfer directly: **PATA-EC**, a domain-general novelty measure that characterises a task by *how it ranks a population of solvers*, and **ANNECS**, a progress metric that counts tasks that were novel, in band against all past solvers, and eventually solved.
**Problem.** POET's novelty measure was hand-coded per domain, its transfer test was noisy and costly, its environment encoding was finite, and open-ended progress had no general metric.
**Method / algorithm.**
- *POET loop:* population of (environment, agent) pairs (40 active). Every M=150 iterations, mutate environments whose agents perform well enough; keep children that pass a **minimal criterion** (not too easy, not too hard for current agents); among those add the most novel (novelty = mean distance to k=5 nearest in active + archive); oldest active moves to the archive when full. Optimise each agent in its environment (ES). Every N=25 iterations, test transfers (goal-switching).
- **PATA-EC:** for an environment, evaluate *all* active and archived agents; clip scores to [lower, upper] (outright failure and competence are uninformative); replace by ranks normalised to [−0.5, 0.5]; Euclidean distance between these vectors is the environment distance. No domain features needed.
- *Improved transfer:* a candidate must beat the max of the incumbent's 5 most recent scores in direct transfer, and only then is fine-tuning transfer computed and must also beat it (cuts false transfers from 50.4% to 22.3% of replacements).
- *CPPN encoding* (NEAT-evolved networks mapping x → terrain height) gives an unbounded environment space.
- **ANNECS:** cumulative count of environments that (1) passed the minimal criterion against all agents ever produced in the run, and (2) were eventually solved.
**Verification / grading.** Environment reward (bipedal walker, solved = finish and score ≥ 230). Not relevant for verifiers.
**Scale & cost.** 60,000 iterations, ~12 days on 750 CPU cores per run; 5 runs.
**Key results.** PATA-EC matches the hand-designed EC's diversity and challenge levels at +82.4% compute; improved transfer reaches the same results with 79.7% of the compute. ANNECS for original POET plateaus after ~20K iterations; Enhanced POET keeps rising almost linearly. Mid- and late-run environments cannot be solved by ES or PPO directly, nor with a hand-made interpolation curriculum, under equal budget (p < 0.01); only POET's stepping stones solve them. Agents are specialists (little cross-environment generalisation).
**Failure modes & limits.** Very expensive; one toy domain; specialists, not generalists; novelty in terms of current population can drift.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Characterise a task by the vector of clipped, rank-normalised scores of a fixed population of solvers.
- Clip out "everyone fails" and "everyone succeeds" before ranking.
- Admit new tasks only if in band against all solvers ever seen, not just the current one.
- Count progress as tasks that were new, in band at birth, and later solved.
- Require transfers (replacements) to beat a recent-max threshold to avoid noise-driven churn.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F7/F8/§3.5: add a **PATA-style behaviour signature from solver rankings**: run a fixed panel of solver configurations (frontier models, the student checkpoints, scaffold variants) on each task in F9/F10, clip and rank-normalise pass rates, and use distance in this space as a "difficulty-profile novelty" complement to the trace signature; two tasks that rank the panel identically teach little that is new. F12/§4 measurements: report an **ANNECS-style curve** for the factory: cumulative tasks that were novel (cell or signature), in band for the student at admission, and later solved by some student checkpoint. Minimal criterion against *all past checkpoints* is a guard against re-admitting already-learned tasks after training. Priority P1 (ANNECS metric P0-cheap).

## 2306.01711 — OMNI: Open-endedness via Models of human Notions of Interestingness (Zhang et al., 2023; ICLR 2024, UBC/Vector)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A, G–O, R skimmed).  Relevance to Part 2: medium–high for F11 and the task loop. Learning progress alone gets stuck on "learnable but boring" variants; an FM judging interestingness (relative to what the agent can already do) fixes it and matches an oracle. Also gives a concrete, reusable learning-progress estimator.
**Problem.** In vast task spaces, learnability filters admit endless trivial variants (numeric repeats, synonyms, compounds), wasting training.
**Method / algorithm.**
- *Learning progress (LP) curriculum:* evaluate success periodically; normalise by a random-policy baseline t_norm = (t_eval − t_rdn)/(1 − t_rdn) (their extension); p_recent = EMA(t_norm, 0.1); p_gradual = EMA(p_recent, 0.1); reweight f(p) = (1−p_θ)p / (p + p_θ(1−2p)) with p_θ = 0.1 (magnifies low-success changes); bidirectional LP = |f(p_recent) − f(p_gradual)| (also catches forgetting); z-score → sigmoid → normalise to sampling probabilities.
- *Model of Interestingness (MoI), finite space:* FM (GPT-3 davinci for Crafter, GPT-4 for BabyAI) few-shot prompted with "learn as many interestingly different skills as possible", the environment description, tasks the agent does well, and candidate tasks; tasks judged boring get weight × 0.001.
- *Infinite space (AI2-THOR):* GPT-4 sees tasks done well (success > 0.6) and tasks failed, reasons, proposes 3 next tasks in natural language **and** as code success conditions (sequences of required object-attribute states); LP runs over the growing set.
**Verification / grading.** Environment reward; in AI2-THOR, success = the FM-written sequence of state predicates is reached in order (executable, state-based). Hackability of FM-written predicates not studied.
**Scale & cost.** Crafter/BabyAI 100M steps × 10 seeds (~33 h / 60 h on one A10); AI2-THOR 1M steps × 10 seeds.
**Key results.** Crafter (15 interesting + 90 boring + 1,023 impossible tasks): tasks learned (success ≥ 0.2) uniform 4, LP 55, OMNI 82; interesting tasks learned 3 / 9 / 14; mean success on interesting tasks 0.10 / 0.52 / 0.78. BabyAI (1,364 tasks): 1 / 4 / 8. FM MoI ≈ oracle MoI (most differences not significant). Robust when boring tasks are compounds or synonyms. AI2-THOR: tasks learned uniform 0, LP 2, OMNI 13. Random-baseline normalisation improves LP.
**Failure modes & limits.** Goodhart risk if a generator is trained to please the MoI (they propose human feedback, "OEHF"); toy environments; FM cost per judgement.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Learnability is necessary but not sufficient; add an "interestingly different from what is mastered" judgement.
- Give the judge the list of mastered and failed tasks; ask for the next task relative to them.
- Down-weight (×0.001) rather than delete boring tasks.
- Normalise success by a trivial-policy baseline before computing learning progress.
- Use bidirectional learning progress to resurface forgotten tasks.
- Have the proposer emit the success condition as executable state predicates with each task.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F11: archive score = learnability (band membership or LP) × interestingness; implement the MoI as a different-family judge given the cell's current elites and the student's mastered tasks, voting "interestingly different / near-copy". F9/F12: adopt the LP estimator for `p̂` dynamics per (task, student, checkpoint) and use bidirectional LP to schedule re-measurement of forgotten tasks; normalise by the null-agent pass rate from G4 (our random-policy analogue). F3: proposals conditioned on mastered/failed lists (OMNI-EPIC extends this). Priority P1.

## 2405.15568 — OMNI-EPIC: Open-endedness via Models of human Notions of Interestingness with Environments Programmed in Code (Faldor, Zhang et al., 2024; ICLR 2025, Imperial/UBC)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–C, G–L; long example appendices D–F, M skimmed).  Relevance to Part 2: high (core self-improving task/env generator). FM writes the next task, the environment code (reset/step/reward/terminated) *and* a separate success-check function; an archive of successes **and failures** conditions the generator; a post-generation interestingness check against retrieved nearest tasks gates admission; ANNECS-OMNI measures progress.
**Problem.** Open-ended environment generators are confined to hand-parameterised spaces; a code-writing generator needs to keep tasks learnable and interesting.
**Method / algorithm.**
1. *Archive:* natural-language description + executable environment code for every task, labelled learned or failed; seeded with 3 descriptions.
2. *Task generator* (Claude 3 Opus, T=0): pick a random archive task, retrieve the 5 most similar learned and 5 most similar failed tasks (embedding cosine over description + code); prompt asks for a novel, useful, specific next task with explicit success condition, reward and penalty components and termination; domain constraints listed (robot cannot grab; obstacles must not be bypassable; target zones non-colliding).
3. *Environment generator* (Claude 3 Opus, 5 few-shot examples) writes Gymnasium-style PyBullet code; compile errors fed back, ≤5 attempts, else regenerate a new task.
4. *Post-generation MoI* (GPT-4o): compare new task code with the 10 most similar archive tasks; admit only if novel, creative, engaging, not too easy given current skills, or useful.
5. *Training:* DreamerV3, 2M steps (~1 h on 2 GPUs per task), initialised from the policy of the most similar learned task.
6. *Success detector:* the environment generator also writes `get_success` (distinct from the shaped reward, so less hackable); VLM detectors were not accurate enough. If not learned, the task goes back for modification (easier world, different reward) up to the attempt limit; then archived as failed.
7. *Metrics:* cell coverage of PCA-projected task embeddings (discretisation 10–50); **ANNECS-OMNI** = ANNECS + the FM judges the task interesting relative to the archive.
**Verification / grading.** Code-based success predicate written by the same model that wrote the task; human study (50 participants) agrees with it 72.7%. Hack surface: generator writes both world and checker, so a trivially true checker passes; nothing like a negative test exists.
**Scale & cost.** Long run: 200 iterations without training ("simulated learning"); short runs: ~25 tasks with training, 5 replications.
**Key results.** Coverage and ANNECS-OMNI significantly higher than controls without archive or without MoI (p<0.05). Short run example: 16 learned, 6 failed, 1 rejected as uninteresting. **Ablations:** uniform (not most-similar) in-context examples → 5 tasks learned vs 16; no failed examples → 7 (generator regenerates already-failed tasks); no learning-progress notion in the prompt → **0 learned**; transfer from nearest policy 63.2% success vs 36.8% from scratch. Fraction of attempted tasks learned decreases over time (tasks get harder).
**Failure modes & limits.** Single simulator; specialists per task; checker and world from the same model; possible training-data recall of environments (authors searched online, found none); possible cyclic rejection.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Condition the generator on the nearest successes *and* nearest failures; drop failures and it repeats them.
- Retrieve most-similar tasks as stepping stones, not random ones.
- State "not too hard for current skills" in the generator prompt; removing it collapses learnability.
- Write a success predicate separate from the shaped reward.
- Gate admission with an interestingness check against the k nearest archive items.
- Repair loop on compile errors (≤5), then discard.
- Warm-start the solver from the nearest learned task.
- Measure progress as tasks that were in band, solved, and judged interesting (ANNECS-OMNI).
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** This is close to the §3.5 item 6 design already cited; concrete adoptions: F3 prompt context = 5 nearest kept tasks + 5 nearest failed/rejected tasks in the cell (with their failure reason from F6/F9), plus an explicit difficulty statement relative to the student's current band; F11 admission = the post-generation interestingness check by a different-family model vs 10 nearest (we already plan this; use their criteria list); F12 = ANNECS-OMNI as the headline factory progress metric. Avoid: success checker written by the same model as the task with no negatives (our F5 negatives + G5/G12 cover this) and single-run claims. Priority P0 (failed-examples context and interestingness gate are cheap and evidenced).

## 2309.16797 — Promptbreeder: Self-Referential Self-Improvement Via Prompt Evolution (Fernando et al., 2023; ICML 2024, Google DeepMind)
Read: FULL (main text, App. A–J; Table 1 numbers partly lost in HTML extraction; APE task appendix K skimmed).  Relevance to Part 2: medium for the §3.8 generator loop (evolving proposer/verifier-writer prompts) and as a cautionary example of self-referential prompt optimisation against a training-set fitness.
**Problem.** Hand-designed prompt strategies are suboptimal and iterative automatic prompt engineering (APE) stalls after ~3 rounds.
**Method / algorithm.**
- *Unit of evolution:* two sequential task-prompts + one mutation-prompt (+ optional few-shot context of correct workings). Population 50; binary tournament GA: sample two units, mutate the fitter, overwrite the loser; 20–30 generations; fitness = accuracy on a random batch of 100 training Q&A.
- *Init:* problem description D + random mutation-prompt + random "thinking style" (e.g. "Let's think step by step") → LLM produces initial task-prompts (diversity from the combinatorial seed sets).
- *Nine operators, one sampled uniformly per replication:* zero-order generation ("A list of 100 hints:" from D, a reset toward the problem); first-order (mutation-prompt + parent); **EDA** (continue a list of current prompts filtered so none is >0.95 BERT-cosine to another, no fitness shown since the LLM just copied); EDA rank-and-index (ascending fitness order but labelled "descending" to avoid copying the last item); lineage (chronological elites of the unit); **hyper-mutation** zero-order (new mutation-prompt from D + thinking style) and first-order ("summarize and improve the following instruction" on the mutation-prompt); **Lamarckian** (reverse-engineer an instruction from a correct working); crossover (10%) and few-shot context shuffling.
**Verification / grading.** Fitness is training-batch accuracy with exact answers; no held-out selection inside the loop beyond random batches.
**Scale & cost.** 50 units × 20–30 generations × 100-question evaluations; PaLM 2-L.
**Key results.** Beats Plan-and-Solve+ (PaLM 2-L) on arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks zero-shot, more few-shot; GSM8K zero-shot 83.9% with the evolved prompt "SOLUTION" (vs OPRO's 80.2%); ETHOS hate speech 89% vs 80% hand prompt. Operator success rates show all classes contribute; removing self-referential operators hurts almost everywhere; one init operator hurt GSM8K. Fitness keeps rising (unlike iterative APE).
**Failure modes & limits.** Fixed prompting topology; evolved prompts can be unintelligible (e.g. "SOLUTION"), a sign of overfitting to the scorer and model; training-set fitness invites overfitting; only prompt content evolves.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Keep a zero-order "reset from the problem description" operator to escape divergence.
- Filter near-duplicate prompts (cosine > 0.95) before showing a population to the mutator.
- Do not show raw fitness numbers; the LLM copies the best.
- Evolve the mutation instructions too, but score them by the improvement they cause.
- Reverse-engineer instructions from successful outputs (Lamarckian step).
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** §3.8 generator loop: Promptbreeder is the mechanism the "pipeline doctor" would use to evolve F3 proposer prompts or F5 verifier-writer prompts. Fitness must be our guarded signals (anchor-set agreement, gate pass rates on a frozen slice, held-out family meta-eval), never student reward, and evolved prompts must be human-readable (reject unintelligible winners like "SOLUTION" as an overfitting flag). Hyper-mutation should stay proposal-only until the anchor set and canaries exist. Priority P2.

## 2402.13064 — Synthetic Data (Almost) from Scratch: Generalized Instruction Tuning for Language Models (Li, Dong et al., 2024; preprint, Microsoft; "GLAN")
Read: FULL (main text; appendix per-skill/discipline tables skimmed).  Relevance to Part 2: medium for F0 and coverage (§3.5): a taxonomy-first generator where the *only* input is a human-verified hierarchy, decomposed by LLMs down to atomic units, then sampled in combinations. Not agentic, no verification.
**Problem.** Instruction data from seeds or existing datasets inherits their narrow coverage.
**Method / algorithm (Alg. 1).**
1. *Taxonomy:* GPT-4 lists fields → sub-fields → disciplines from several prompts; humans vote keep/remove (removing a node removes descendants); 126 disciplines kept.
2. *Subjects:* per discipline, GPT-4 as education expert lists subjects with level and subtopics; second prompt converts to JSONL (formatting in a separate call keeps content quality); 10 queries per discipline (T=1.0, top-p 0.95) → 100–200 subjects each; cross-discipline duplicates kept as importance signal.
3. *Syllabus:* one GPT-4 call per subject → 10–30 class sessions × ~5 key concepts; extract sessions and concepts by another prompt.
4. *Instructions:* sample 1 session + 1–5 key concepts (basic) or 2 sessions + 2–5 concepts spanning both (harder; combinatorially more), prompt GPT-4 for a homework question assuming the student learned up to those sessions; answers by GPT-3.5-turbo separately (T=0.7).
5. Decontaminate against benchmark train and test prompts; 10M pairs; SFT Mistral-7B 3 epochs.
**Verification / grading.** None for answers (GPT-3.5 trusted). Contamination check via train/test loss gap on ARC, GSM8K, MATH (no gap for GLAN).
**Scale & cost.** 10M instruction–response pairs; GPT-4 for structure and questions, GPT-3.5 for answers; cost not stated.
**Key results.** GLAN-7B (Mistral base, no task training sets): HumanEval 48.8, BBH 60.7, ARC-E 90.7, ARC-C 81.1, MMLU 62.9 (other cells in Table 1 lost in extraction); gains concentrate on STEM (CoT-style answers); weaker on humanities and on some disciplines (American history, divinity, radiology). Built a 6,300-item held-out GLAN-Test across 126 disciplines.
**Failure modes & limits.** Answer correctness unverified; QA only; taxonomy reflects education structure, not usage frequency.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Taxonomy as the only seed; humans verify only the top levels (cheap).
- Decompose to atomic units (session, key concept) and sample combinations for coverage and difficulty.
- Cross-unit combinations are the harder tier.
- Separate content generation from format conversion.
- Keep a held-out set generated by the same taxonomy for breadth evaluation.
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F0 DomainSpec: the skill tree should be built GLAN-style (LLM proposes hierarchy, human prunes top levels; leaves decomposed automatically into skill leaves with observable signatures); combination sampling of 1 vs 2 leaves maps to single-skill vs composed tasks (knob "composition"). Coverage meter = fraction of leaves and leaf pairs with kept tasks. Add a usage-weighting step (Daedalus surveyor, trace clusters) because the taxonomy alone weights by structure. Priority P1.

## 2406.20094 — Scaling Synthetic Data Creation with 1,000,000,000 Personas (Ge et al., 2024; tech report, Tencent AI Lab; "Persona Hub")
Read: FULL.  Relevance to Part 2: medium. Personas are the cheapest exogenous diversity source for F3 and the natural root for F2 people/universe seeding (ARE already roots Mobile universes in Persona Hub). The quantitative finding that output similarity is correlated with but lower than persona similarity, and rises when the prompt is constrained, matters for how much diversity personas buy inside a narrow cell.
**Problem.** Scaling synthetic data quantity is easy; scaling its diversity needs many distinct prompts, which seed corpora and key-point lists cannot supply.
**Method / algorithm.**
- *Text-to-Persona:* prompt an LLM with any web text (RedPajama v2): "who is likely to read / write / like / dislike this?"; ask for specific personas; detailed texts yield fine-grained personas.
- *Persona-to-Persona:* "who is in close relationship with this persona?" for six iterations to reach low-web-visibility people (a child via a paediatric nurse; a beggar via a shelter worker).
- *Dedup:* MinHash on 1-grams (signature 128, threshold 0.9), then embedding cosine > 0.9 removed (tighter, e.g. 0.5, if fewer but more diverse personas are needed); heuristic quality filter → 1,015,863,523 personas.
- *Use:* insert a persona into any synthesis prompt (zero-shot, few-shot, or persona-enhanced few-shot where demonstrations' personas are inferred too). Demonstrated: math and logic problems, user instructions (incl. WildChat-styled few-shot), knowledge-rich texts, game NPCs, tool definitions → implementations.
**Verification / grading.** Math solutions by GPT-4o; in-distribution test kept only where ≥2 of 3 solvers agree; expert check of 200 hard problems: 96.5% valid (7 invalid for insufficient/conflicting conditions).
**Scale & cost.** 1.09M math problems from 1.09M personas (GPT-4, zero-shot); releases 200K personas and samples; later 370M expert personas.
**Key results.** Qwen2-7B fine-tuned on 1.07M persona problems: MATH 64.9% (OOD; matches gpt-4-turbo-preview), synthetic in-distribution test 79.4%; scaling curve rises with data. Problem-pair similarity < persona-pair similarity across 0.4/0.6/0.8 bins; constraining the topic (finance + probability) raises problem similarity; at persona similarity 0.9, GPT-4o and GPT-3.5 problems have similarity mostly 0.6–0.75.
**Failure modes & limits.** Personas are 1–2 sentences, coarse; hallucinated content; data-extraction risk acknowledged; no verification of most outputs.
**Tips & tricks.**
- Derive personas from real text ("who would read/write this") plus relationship expansion for hidden populations.
- Dedup personas by MinHash then embeddings; tune the threshold to the diversity you need.
- Expect topic constraints to shrink persona-induced diversity; measure it per cell.
- Infer personas for few-shot demonstrations so style follows real users.
- Use personas to predict tools users would need (tool-pool design).
**Fit to Crucible Part 2.** F2 people-as-data: sample `ActorSpec`s from a persona pool (persona → relationships → contacts/colleagues), seeded per universe as in ARE; enterprise variant: derive personas from redacted org documents via text-to-persona. F3: persona is one of the three grounding inputs (cell, World slice, persona); because cell constraints raise similarity, rely on behaviour signatures (F7), not persona variety, to certify diversity. F1: persona-driven "what tools would this user need" is a cheap App-surface brainstorming step for a new DomainSpec. Priority P1.
